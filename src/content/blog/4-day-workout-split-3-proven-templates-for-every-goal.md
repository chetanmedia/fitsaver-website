A **4 day workout split** means lifting four days a week, with each session focused on different muscles or a different workout, so every major muscle gets trained about twice a week and you still get three rest days. It’s the sweet spot for most people who lift: enough sessions to train every muscle twice a week, and enough rest days to recover and keep a life outside the gym. The hard part is not finding a split. It’s picking one that fits your goal and then following it for more than two weeks. Below are three proven templates, one for strength, one for muscle size and one for general fitness, each with a day-by-day routine card you can copy.

## Why a 4 day workout split works for most lifters

Four training days give you room to hit each major muscle group about twice a week while keeping three days free for recovery, work and everything else. That lines up well with public health guidance: the [CDC recommends](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) at least two days of muscle-strengthening activity each week for adults, and the [NHS suggests](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/physical-activity-guidelines-for-adults-aged-19-to-64/) working all the major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms) on at least two days, with activity spread across the week.

### Who a workout split 4 day plan is for

- **Beginners after their first few months** who have outgrown a basic full body plan and want more focused sessions.
- **Intermediate lifters** who want steady progress without living in the gym.
- **Busy people** who can reliably train four days but not five or six.
- **Anyone switching goals**, since the same four-day frame works for strength, muscle or general fitness.

### How to choose the best 4 day workout split for you

Start with your main goal, then check your schedule. If you want to get stronger on the big lifts, pick the upper/lower template. If you mainly want bigger muscles and enjoy longer, focused sessions, pick the body-part template. If you want to feel fitter, move better and keep training simple, pick the full body A/B template. Not sure how a 4-day plan compares with 3, 5 or 6 days? Our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) walks through every option.

## 4 day split at a glance: which template fits you?

All three templates use the same week: train Monday and Tuesday, rest Wednesday, train Thursday and Friday, and rest over the weekend. What changes is how the muscles are grouped.

| Template | Monday | Tuesday | Thursday | Friday | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upper/lower | Upper A (heavy) | Lower A (heavy) | Upper B (volume) | Lower B (volume) | Getting stronger on the big lifts |
| Body-part | Chest and triceps | Back and biceps | Legs and core | Shoulders and arms | Muscle size, longer focused sessions |
| Full body A/B | Full body A | Full body B | Full body A | Full body B | General fitness, simpler sessions |

If you can’t decide, start with upper/lower. It’s the most common 4 day split for a reason: it trains everything twice a week, keeps sessions to a reasonable length and is easy to progress.

## Template 1: Upper/lower 4 day split for strength

The upper/lower split is the classic best 4 day workout split for strength. You train upper body twice and lower body twice, with one heavier day and one lighter, higher-rep day for each. Heavy compound lifts come first while you’re fresh, followed by accessories.

### Weekly schedule

- **Monday:** Upper A (heavy)
- **Tuesday:** Lower A (heavy)
- **Wednesday:** Rest or a walk
- **Thursday:** Upper B (volume)
- **Friday:** Lower B (volume)
- **Weekend:** Rest

![Woman performing a barbell back squat in a gym, a core lift on the lower days of a 4 day upper/lower split](https://framerusercontent.com/images/J4AIKUm0haQJW3XZGH11Fz18.jpg)

### Routine card: Upper A

1. Barbell bench press: 4 sets of 5 reps, rest 2 to 3 min
2. Barbell row: 4 sets of 6 reps, rest 2 min
3. Overhead press: 3 sets of 6 reps, rest 2 min
4. Lat pulldown: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 sec
5. Dumbbell curl: 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec
6. Triceps pushdown: 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec

### Routine card: Lower A

1. Barbell back squat: 4 sets of 5 reps, rest 2 to 3 min
2. Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 6 to 8 reps, rest 2 min
3. Walking lunge: 3 sets of 10 steps per leg, rest 90 sec
4. Leg curl: 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec
5. Standing calf raise: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 sec
6. Plank: 3 holds of 30 to 45 sec, rest 45 sec

**Upper B and Lower B** use the same movement patterns with lighter loads and more reps: incline dumbbell press, seated cable row, dumbbell shoulder press and pull-ups for upper; leg press, deadlift or hip thrust, split squat and hanging knee raise for lower. Aim for 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest.

## Template 2: Body-part 4 day split for muscle size

If your goal is muscle size, a body-part split lets you put more sets into each muscle per session. Each muscle still gets hit directly once and indirectly a second time, because pressing days also work shoulders and triceps and pulling days also work biceps and rear delts.

### Weekly schedule

- **Monday:** Chest and triceps
- **Tuesday:** Back and biceps
- **Wednesday:** Rest
- **Thursday:** Legs and core
- **Friday:** Shoulders and arms
- **Weekend:** Rest or light cardio

### Routine card: Chest and triceps

1. Incline dumbbell press: 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 sec
2. Flat barbell or machine press: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 sec
3. Cable fly: 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 60 sec
4. Dips or close-grip bench press: 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps, rest 90 sec
5. Overhead cable triceps extension: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 sec

Want more ideas for the first exercise of the day? Our [upper chest workout](/blog/upper-chest-workout) breaks down the incline moves in detail.

### Routine card: Back and biceps

1. Pull-ups or lat pulldown: 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 sec
2. Chest-supported dumbbell row: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 sec
3. Seated cable row: 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec
4. Face pull: 3 sets of 15 reps, rest 60 sec
5. Incline dumbbell curl: 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec

**Legs and core** pairs squats or leg press with Romanian deadlifts, lunges, leg curls and calf raises, then finishes with two core moves. **Shoulders and arms** opens with a seated dumbbell press, then lateral raises, rear-delt flyes and a curl and triceps superset. Keep most sets in the 8 to 15 rep range and stop one or two reps short of failure.

## Template 3: Full body A/B split for general fitness

This template trains your whole body every session, alternating two workouts: A, B, A, B. It’s the easiest 4 day workout split to stick to when life gets busy, because missing one day never means a muscle group goes untrained for a week. Each session ends with a short conditioning finisher.

### Weekly schedule

- **Monday:** Full body A
- **Tuesday:** Full body B
- **Wednesday:** Rest
- **Thursday:** Full body A
- **Friday:** Full body B
- **Weekend:** Rest, walk or easy bike ride

![Lifter gripping a loaded barbell on a rubber gym floor before a deadlift, part of a full body workout](https://framerusercontent.com/images/wbb3HWci62Uehu8qoMmHPeU9qiM.jpg)

### Routine card: Full body A

1. Goblet squat: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 sec
2. Dumbbell bench press: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 sec
3. One-arm dumbbell row: 3 sets of 10 reps per side, rest 60 sec
4. Glute bridge: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 sec
5. Finisher: 4 rounds of 30 sec kettlebell swings and 30 sec rest

### Routine card: Full body B

1. Trap bar or dumbbell deadlift: 3 sets of 8 reps, rest 2 min
2. Standing dumbbell shoulder press: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 sec
3. Lat pulldown: 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 sec
4. Reverse lunge: 3 sets of 8 per leg, rest 60 sec
5. Finisher: 4 rounds of 20 sec rowing or bike sprint and 40 sec easy pace

## Can you move the training days around?

Yes. The Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday pattern is popular because it puts a rest day in the middle of the week, but any four days work if you follow two rules: never run two hard lower-body days back to back, and get at least one full rest day between each pair of sessions when you can.

- **Weekend lifters:** Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday.
- **Shift workers:** any two pairs of back-to-back days with a rest day between the pairs.
- **4 days on, 1 day off:** possible with the full body A/B template if sessions stay moderate, but most people recover better with two or three rest days spread across the week.

Missed a day? Don’t cram two sessions into one. Push the rest of the week back a day and keep the order of the workouts the same.

## How to progress on any 4 day workout split

The template matters less than steady progress. Pick one and run it for at least six to eight weeks before judging it.

- **Use double progression.** When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add a small amount of weight next session and work back up.
- **Log every session.** Writing down weights and reps is the simplest way to know when to add load.
- **Keep form first.** Add weight only when every rep looks the same as the first one.
- **Deload when you need it.** Every sixth to eighth week, or when you feel run down, cut sets in half for a week.
- **Protect your rest days.** Rest is when you recover. A walk or light mobility is fine; another hard session is not.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- Switching templates every couple of weeks before you’ve had time to progress.
- Adding extra exercises until sessions run past 75 minutes.
- Skipping legs on the busy weeks (the full body template fixes this).
- Putting two heavy lower days back to back with no rest between them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 4 day workout split good for beginners?

Yes, once you have a few months of consistent training behind you. If you’re brand new, start with the full body A/B template, since it uses simpler lifts and trains everything each session.

### Which is the best 4 day workout split?

The best one is the one that matches your goal and that you can repeat for months. Upper/lower suits strength, the body-part split suits muscle size and full body A/B suits general fitness and busy schedules.

### How long should each session take?

Plan for 45 to 75 minutes including warm-up. If sessions regularly run longer, trim accessory sets before cutting the main lifts.

### Can I do cardio on a 4 day split?

Yes. Add easy cardio on rest days or after lifting, and keep hard interval work away from the day before a heavy lower-body session.

### Is a 4 day split enough to build muscle?

Yes. Four sessions a week trains every major muscle about twice, which is plenty for steady muscle growth as long as you add weight or reps over time, eat enough protein and sleep well. The body-part template puts a little more volume on each muscle if size is your main goal.

### Can I train on consecutive days?

Yes. Every template here uses two days on, one day off, two days on, with each back-to-back pair training different muscles or alternating workouts.
