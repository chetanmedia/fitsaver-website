A good glute workout does three things: it trains your hips through extension (driving them forward), it includes at least one single-leg move, and it adds a little side-to-side work for the smaller glute muscles. Do that two or three times a week, add weight or reps over time, and your glutes get stronger whether you train in a gym or a living room. This guide covers the eight best glute exercises, a gym and a home version of each, and a routine card you can run starting today.

## What your glutes actually do

Your glutes are three muscles, and each one has a slightly different job. The gluteus maximus is the big one: it extends your hip, which is the movement of standing up from a squat, climbing a step or pushing your hips forward at the top of a bridge. The gluteus medius and gluteus minimus sit higher and to the side. They move your leg out to the side and keep your pelvis level when you stand on one leg.

That’s why the best glute workout isn’t just squats. Squats train the glutes well, but a complete plan covers:

- **Hip extension:** hip thrusts, glute bridges and Romanian deadlifts.
- **Single-leg strength:** step-ups, lunges and split squats, which also challenge the side glutes to keep you balanced.
- **Abduction:** banded walks and side-lying leg raises for the gluteus medius.

## What the research says about the best glute exercises

Two pieces of research are worth knowing. An [American Council on Exercise study](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/9061/glute-goals-an-evidence-based-glute-workout/) measured muscle activity in 12 people across eight common lower-body exercises. Quadruped hip extensions and squats produced the highest gluteus maximus activity, while step-ups, lunges and quadruped hip extensions worked the gluteus medius harder than squats did. Both leg press variations came last for glute activity.

A 2020 [systematic review in the Journal of Sports Science and Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7039033/) pulled together the wider research on gluteus maximus activation. Step-ups and their variations came out on top, followed by bilateral lifts such as deadlifts, hip thrusts and squats. The authors are careful to call the ranking tentative, because studies measure muscle activity in different ways.

The practical takeaway: there is no single magic glute exercise. A mix of a hip hinge, a hip thrust or bridge, a single-leg move and some side work covers everything the studies point to.

## The 8 best glute exercises

Each move below has a gym version and a home version. Pick whichever matches your setup.

### 1. Hip thrust

Sit with your upper back against a bench, feet flat and about hip-width apart. Drive through your heels, push your hips up until your torso is level with your thighs, squeeze your glutes for a second, then lower with control. Keep your chin tucked and your ribs down so the work stays in your hips, not your lower back.

- **Gym:** barbell hip thrust with a pad across your hips.
- **Home:** hip thrust off a couch or sturdy chair, with a backpack or dumbbell on your hips.

### 2. Glute bridge

The glute bridge is the hip thrust’s floor version. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, press through your heels and lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees. It’s a great warm-up, a beginner starting point and a high-rep finisher.

- **Gym:** weighted bridge with a dumbbell or plate on your hips.
- **Home:** single-leg glute bridge, with one foot lifted, to make bodyweight harder.

### 3. Romanian deadlift

Stand tall holding weights in front of your thighs. Push your hips back with a soft bend in your knees, sliding the weights down your legs until you feel a strong stretch in your hamstrings, then drive your hips forward to stand up. Keep your back flat and the weights close to your body.

- **Gym:** barbell or dumbbell Romanian deadlift.
- **Home:** single-leg Romanian deadlift holding a water jug or backpack.

### 4. Step-up

Place one whole foot on a box or bench at about knee height. Drive through that heel to stand up on the box without pushing off the back foot, then step down slowly. Leaning your torso slightly forward shifts more of the work into your glutes.

- **Gym:** dumbbell step-up onto a plyo box or flat bench.
- **Home:** step-up onto a sturdy step, stair or low chair.

### 5. Reverse lunge

Step one foot back and lower until both knees are bent to roughly 90 degrees, then push through the front heel to return. Reverse lunges are easier on the knees than forward lunges for most people, and they let you keep your weight in the front hip.

- **Gym:** dumbbell reverse lunge, or a Bulgarian split squat with your back foot on a bench.
- **Home:** bodyweight reverse lunge, slowed to a three-second lowering.

### 6. Quadruped hip extension

Start on your hands and knees. Keeping your knee bent at 90 degrees, lift one leg until your thigh is level with your torso, as if pressing your foot toward the ceiling, then lower. Keep your back flat and don’t twist. This is the move many people call a donkey kick.

- **Gym:** cable kickback with an ankle strap.
- **Home:** donkey kick with a resistance band looped around your foot and hands.

![Woman doing a kneeling glute kickback on an exercise mat outdoors](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1588482524100-2b3c0d36e348?crop=entropy&cs=srgb&fm=jpg&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1600&auto=format)

### 7. Goblet squat

Hold one dumbbell or kettlebell at your chest, feet a little wider than hip-width, and sit down between your heels as low as you can while keeping your chest up. Stand by driving the floor away. Squatting a little deeper, with control, puts more of the load on the glutes.

- **Gym:** goblet squat, or a barbell back squat once your form is solid.
- **Home:** tempo bodyweight squat with a pause at the bottom.

### 8. Banded lateral walk

Loop a mini band above your knees or around your ankles, sink into a quarter squat and take small steps to the side, keeping tension on the band the whole time. This is the simplest way to train the gluteus medius, the side glute that keeps your knees and hips stable on every single-leg move above.

- **Gym:** cable or machine hip abduction.
- **Home:** banded lateral walk, or side-lying leg raises with no equipment.

## How to structure a glute workout

You don’t need all eight exercises in every session. A strong glute workout usually has four to six moves in this order:

1. **One activation move** (bridge or lateral walk) to warm up the hips.
2. **One heavy hip-extension lift** (hip thrust or Romanian deadlift) while you’re fresh.
3. **One single-leg move** (step-up or reverse lunge).
4. **One or two lighter finishers** (kickbacks, abduction or a high-rep bridge).

For sets and reps, three to four sets of 8 to 12 reps on the main lifts and 12 to 20 reps on the finishers works for most goals. Rest about 60 to 90 seconds on the heavy moves and 30 to 45 seconds on the light ones.

### How often to train glutes

Two or three glute sessions a week, with at least a day between them, suits most people. That fits the [CDC’s guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) of muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days a week. If you follow a split, glute work slots naturally into leg or lower-body days; our [5 day workout split for muscle gain](/blog/5-day-workout-split-for-muscle-gain) shows one way to fit it in, and the [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) helps you pick the right number of days.

### How to progress

Glutes respond to gradual overload like any other muscle. Each week, try one of these:

- Add one or two reps per set until you reach the top of the rep range.
- Then add a little weight and drop back to the bottom of the range.
- At home, slow the lowering phase, add a pause at the top or switch to a single-leg version.

Logging what you lift is what makes this work. If you can’t remember last week’s weight, you can’t beat it.

## Glute workout routine card

Run this two or three times a week. It takes about 40 minutes, including a short warm-up.

1. **Banded lateral walk:** 2 sets of 12 steps each way, 30 seconds rest
2. **Hip thrust:** 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps, 90 seconds rest
3. **Romanian deadlift:** 3 sets of 10 reps, 90 seconds rest
4. **Step-up:** 3 sets of 10 reps per leg, 60 seconds rest
5. **Quadruped hip extension:** 3 sets of 15 reps per leg, 30 seconds rest
6. **Glute bridge burnout:** 1 set of 20 to 25 reps, done

## Common glute workout mistakes

- **Only doing squats.** Squats are great, but adding a hinge, a single-leg move and some side work covers the parts squats miss.
- **Arching the lower back at the top.** On hip thrusts and bridges, stop when your hips are level and squeeze your glutes. Over-arching moves the work into your back.
- **Rushing the reps.** Control the lowering on every move. A slow two- or three-second descent makes lighter weights far more effective.
- **Never adding load.** If your glute workout looks the same as it did two months ago, your results probably do too.
- **Skipping rest.** Muscles adapt between sessions. If you need ideas for those days, read our guide to [rest days](/blog/rest-days-what-to-do-and-how-many-you-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best exercise for glutes?

There isn’t one single best exercise. Research ranks step-ups, hip thrusts, deadlifts, squats and quadruped hip extensions near the top for gluteus maximus activity. A glute workout that combines a hip thrust or bridge, a hinge, a single-leg move and some side work covers all three glute muscles.

### How many times a week should I do a glute workout?

Two or three sessions a week, with at least one rest day between them, works well for most people. Beginners can start with two and add a third once recovery feels easy.

### Can you build glutes at home without weights?

Yes, especially as a beginner. Single-leg bridges, step-ups, slow reverse lunges, donkey kicks and banded walks all train the glutes hard. To keep progressing, add reps, slow the tempo, use one leg at a time, or add a loaded backpack or resistance bands.

### How long does it take to see results from glute workouts?

Strength usually improves within the first few weeks as you learn the movements. Visible changes in muscle size take longer, often a few months of consistent training with gradual overload, and they depend on your starting point, nutrition and sleep.

### Should glute workouts be heavy or high reps?

Both have a place. Use moderate to heavy weights for 8 to 12 reps on the main lifts like hip thrusts and Romanian deadlifts, then lighter, higher-rep finishers for kickbacks and abduction. Mixing rep ranges keeps training varied.
