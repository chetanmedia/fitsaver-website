A strong back workout needs two kinds of pulling: vertical pulls, like pull-ups and lat pulldowns, which build width through your lats, and horizontal pulls, like rows, which build thickness through your mid-back. Add one move for the lower back and one for the rear shoulders and you’ve covered every major back muscle. This guide covers the seven best back exercises, gym and home versions of each, and a routine card you can use starting today.

## The muscles of your back

Your back isn’t one muscle, and that’s why one exercise isn’t enough:

- **Latissimus dorsi (lats):** the large muscles along the sides of your back. They pull your arms down and back.
- **Trapezius (middle and lower):** run across your upper and middle back and move your shoulder blades.
- **Rhomboids:** sit between your shoulder blades and squeeze them together.
- **Rear deltoids:** the back of your shoulders.
- **Erector spinae:** run along your spine and keep your torso upright.

## What the research says about the best back exercises

An [American Council on Exercise study](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/january-2023/8214/what-is-the-best-back-exercise/) measured muscle activity across eight common back exercises in 19 resistance-trained men. A few results stand out:

- **Pull-ups and chin-ups** activated the lats most.
- **The bent-over row** activated the middle trapezius and erector spinae to the greatest degree.
- **I-Y-T raises** targeted the lower trapezius well.

The researchers’ conclusion: no single exercise works every back muscle best. If you could only do one, they picked the bent-over row. If you can do a few, combine a vertical pull, a row and a little shoulder-blade work.

## The 7 best back exercises

### 1. Pull-up or chin-up

Hang from a bar with an overhand grip (pull-up) or underhand grip (chin-up), and pull until your chin clears the bar. Lower with control.

- **Gym:** bodyweight or weighted pull-ups; assisted pull-up machine if needed.
- **Home:** a doorway pull-up bar, or slow negatives (jump up, lower for 3 to 5 seconds).

### 2. Bent-over row

Hinge forward with a flat back, knees slightly bent, and pull the weight toward your lower ribs. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top.

- **Gym:** barbell bent-over row.
- **Home:** dumbbell or backpack row, or a resistance band row.

### 3. Single-arm dumbbell row

Place one knee and hand on a bench, hold a dumbbell in the other hand and row it toward your hip. Working one side at a time helps fix imbalances.

- **Gym:** dumbbell row on a flat bench.
- **Home:** one hand on a chair or couch, rowing a heavy bag or jug.

### 4. Lat pulldown

Sit at the machine, grip the bar a little wider than shoulder-width and pull it to your upper chest, driving your elbows down.

- **Gym:** cable lat pulldown.
- **Home:** band pulldown with the band anchored over a door.

### 5. Seated cable row

Sit tall, pull the handle to your stomach and squeeze your shoulder blades back without leaning far backward.

- **Gym:** seated cable row with a V-handle.
- **Home:** sit with legs straight, loop a band around your feet and row.

### 6. Inverted row

Lie under a bar or sturdy table, grab the edge and pull your chest up to it with your body straight. It’s a great bridge toward pull-ups.

- **Gym:** inverted row in a Smith machine or squat rack.
- **Home:** under a sturdy table, holding the edge.

### 7. I-Y-T raise

Lie face down on an incline bench or the floor and raise your arms in an I, then a Y, then a T shape, thumbs up. Use light weights or none at all.

- **Gym:** with light dumbbells on an incline bench.
- **Home:** face down on the floor with no weight.

## How to structure a back workout

A complete back workout usually follows this order:

1. **One vertical pull** (pull-up or lat pulldown) while you’re fresh.
2. **One heavy row** (bent-over row).
3. **One unilateral or machine row** (single-arm row or cable row).
4. **One shoulder-blade or rear-delt move** (I-Y-T raise or face pull).

Three to four sets of 6 to 12 reps on the big pulls and 12 to 15 reps on the smaller moves works for most goals. Rest 90 seconds to 2 minutes on the heavy lifts and about 60 seconds on the rest.

### How often to train your back

Twice a week suits most people, and it fits the [CDC’s recommendation](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) of muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days a week. ### A simpler back workout for beginners

New to back training? Start with three moves, twice a week:

1. **Lat pulldown or band pulldown:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps.
2. **Single-arm dumbbell row:** 3 sets of 10 reps per side.
3. **Inverted row with bent knees:** 2 sets of 8 to 10 reps.

Use weights that leave two or three reps in reserve, focus on squeezing your shoulder blades, and add the bent-over row once your hip hinge feels solid.

### Form tips that make every back exercise better

- **Lead with your elbows.** Think about pulling your elbows back or down, not your hands.
- **Squeeze at the top** of every rep for a second.
- **Control the lowering.** Don’t let the weight drop.
- **Keep your back flat** on bent-over moves. If your lower back rounds, the weight is too heavy.
- **Don’t use momentum.** Swinging takes the work off your back.

## Back workout routine card

Run this once or twice a week. It takes about 45 minutes.

1. **Pull-up or lat pulldown:** 4 sets of 6 to 10 reps, 2 minutes rest
2. **Barbell bent-over row:** 4 sets of 8 reps, 2 minutes rest
3. **Single-arm dumbbell row:** 3 sets of 10 reps per side, 90 seconds rest
4. **Seated cable row:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
5. **I-Y-T raise:** 2 sets of 8 reps per letter, 60 seconds rest

Training at home? Swap the pull-up for slow negatives or band pulldowns, the bent-over row for a backpack row, the cable row for a band row, and add inverted rows under a sturdy table.

## Common back workout mistakes

- **Only doing pulldowns.** You need rows for thickness and mid-back strength too.
- **Pulling with your arms.** If your biceps burn before your back, focus on driving the elbows.
- **Rounding on bent-over rows.** Keep the spine neutral and use a weight you can control.
- **Skipping rear delts and lower traps.** Small moves like I-Y-T raises keep your shoulders balanced.
- **Never progressing.** Add a rep or a little weight whenever you hit the top of your rep range.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best exercise for your back?

There isn’t one that works every back muscle best. In ACE-sponsored research, pull-ups and chin-ups activated the lats most, and the bent-over row was the best all-rounder. Combining a vertical pull, a row and some shoulder-blade work covers everything.

### How many times a week should I train my back?

Twice a week works well for most people. Leave at least a day between back sessions so the muscles can recover.

### Can I build my back at home without equipment?

Yes, to a point. Inverted rows under a table, backpack rows, slow pull-up negatives and band rows all build strength. A doorway pull-up bar and a set of bands make home back training much more effective.

### What if I can’t do a pull-up yet?

Start with inverted rows, band-assisted pull-ups or slow negatives where you lower yourself from the top for 3 to 5 seconds. Most people build their first pull-up within a few weeks to a few months of consistent practice.

### How many back exercises should I do in one workout?

Four or five is plenty: a vertical pull, one or two rows, and a small move for the rear shoulders or lower traps.
