A good **ab workout for men** doesn’t need two hundred crunches or a fancy machine. It needs a handful of moves that train the core the way it actually works: curling, lifting the legs, bracing and resisting rotation. This guide gives you two complete versions of the same plan, one for the gym and one for home, plus the sets, reps and rest to run each one, how often to train abs, and how to keep the routine on track when you mostly learn workouts from reels.

## What an ab workout for men should actually train

Your “abs” are more than the six-pack muscle. The rectus abdominis flexes the spine, the obliques rotate and side-bend the torso, and the deeper transverse abdominis braces your midsection when you lift, run or carry. A routine that only does crunches trains one of those jobs and ignores the rest.

That’s why a balanced plan covers four movement patterns:

- **Flexion:** curling the ribs toward the hips (crunches, cable crunches).
- **Leg raises:** lifting the legs while the pelvis tilts up (hanging knee raises, reverse crunches).
- **Anti-extension:** keeping the lower back from arching (planks, ab wheel rollouts, dead bugs).
- **Rotation and anti-rotation:** twisting under control or resisting a twist (bicycle crunches, Pallof presses, side planks).

An [American Council on Exercise study of 13 ab exercises](https://www.acefitness.org/about-ace/press-room/press-releases/246/ace-sponsored-study-reveals-best-and-worst-abdominal-exercises/) ranked the bicycle maneuver, the captain’s chair and the crunch on an exercise ball as the top three overall. Both versions below lean on those movement types, and our [ranked list of the best ab exercises](/blog/best-ab-workout-exercises-ranked) explains where every other common move lands.

### Why men often stall on ab training

Most stalls come from three habits: doing the same easy crunch variation forever, rushing reps with momentum, and training abs only when there’s time left at the end of a session. Treat abs like any other muscle group. Pick variations that are hard enough, control every rep, and add reps, load or time from week to week.

## The gym ab workout for men

The gym version uses a pull-up bar or captain’s chair, a cable stack and an ab wheel. It takes about 20 to 25 minutes and fits at the end of an upper or lower body day.

### 1. Hanging knee raise or leg raise

Hang from a bar (or brace on a captain’s chair), pull your knees toward your chest and curl the pelvis up at the top. Lower slowly without swinging. Once 15 controlled knee raises feel easy, straighten your legs for a harder lever.

![Man performing a hanging knee raise on gymnastic rings in a gym](https://framerusercontent.com/images/qPXKw1cfZ1in3Q5FFwxIYzPnPo.jpg)

### 2. Kneeling cable crunch

Kneel facing a high pulley with a rope attachment held beside your head. Keep your hips still and crunch your ribs down toward your hips. This is the easiest ab move to load progressively: add one plate when you reach the top of the rep range.

### 3. Ab wheel rollout

From your knees, roll the wheel forward as far as you can without your lower back sagging, then pull back using your abs, not your hips. Start with a short range and extend it as you get stronger.

### 4. Pallof press

Stand side-on to a cable set at chest height, hold the handle at your sternum and press it straight out. Hold for two seconds while you resist the cable pulling you into rotation. Train both sides.

### 5. Weighted plank

Finish with a plank, and add a plate on your upper back once a 60-second hold feels easy. [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/want-a-stronger-core-skip-the-sit-ups) describes planks as the gold standard for working your core, and they build the bracing you need for heavy squats and deadlifts.

## The home ab workout for men (no equipment)

The home version swaps machines for bodyweight moves that train the same four patterns. All you need is a floor and, if you have one, a mat. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

### 1. Reverse crunch

Lie on your back with your knees bent at 90 degrees. Curl your hips off the floor toward your ribs, then lower slowly. It’s the home stand-in for hanging leg raises.

### 2. Bicycle crunch

Bring one elbow toward the opposite knee while the other leg extends, then switch. Go slowly and rotate through your ribs rather than pulling on your neck. This is the move that topped the ACE ranking.

### 3. Dead bug

Lie on your back with arms pointing up and knees over hips. Press your lower back into the floor and extend the opposite arm and leg, then switch. If your back starts to arch, shorten the range.

![Two people holding a high plank on exercise mats in a living room](https://framerusercontent.com/images/Muq3QWkJRAFPFkbQEPlEp4pqb6U.jpg)

### 4. Side plank

Stack or stagger your feet, lift your hips into a straight line and hold. Side planks train the obliques and the side-to-side stability that keeps your torso steady when you carry something in one hand.

### 5. Plank shoulder tap

From a high plank with your feet wide, tap one shoulder at a time while keeping your hips from rocking. Slow taps are harder than fast ones.

### Making the home version harder

- Slow the lowering phase to three or four seconds.
- Add a pause at the hardest point of each rep.
- Hold a loaded backpack or a water jug during dead bugs and reverse crunches.
- Swap reverse crunches for lying straight-leg raises once 20 reps feel easy.

## How often should men train abs?

Two or three ab sessions a week is plenty for most people. The [CDC’s physical activity guidelines for adults](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) recommend muscle-strengthening activities that work all major muscle groups, including the abdomen, on at least two days a week, so twice a week is a sensible floor.

Fit ab work into the split you already run. On a push/pull/legs or upper/lower plan, add the routine to the end of two or three sessions rather than giving abs a day of their own. Our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) shows how 3, 4, 5 and 6-day schedules are laid out, so you can pick the days that make sense.

### A simple weekly layout

- **3-day full body:** gym ab workout after sessions one and three.
- **4-day upper/lower:** ab workout after both lower body days.
- **Training at home some weeks:** run the home version on the same days so the habit doesn’t break.

### A note on visible abs

Ab training builds the muscles, but how visible they are depends mostly on overall body fat, which comes down to nutrition, total activity and sleep over months. Treat this routine as strength work for your midsection and tie your expectations about definition to your overall plan.

## Your ab workout routine card

Here is the full plan in one place. Run the gym version when you have equipment and the home version when you don’t.

### Gym version

1. Hanging knee raise or leg raise: 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps, rest 60 seconds
2. Kneeling cable crunch: 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 60 seconds
3. Ab wheel rollout: 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps, rest 60 seconds
4. Pallof press: 3 sets of 10 reps per side with a 2-second hold, rest 45 seconds
5. Weighted plank: 2 sets of 30 to 60 seconds, rest 60 seconds

### Home version

1. Reverse crunch: 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 45 seconds
2. Bicycle crunch: 3 sets of 20 reps (10 per side), rest 45 seconds
3. Dead bug: 3 sets of 10 reps per side, rest 45 seconds
4. Side plank: 2 sets of 30 to 45 seconds per side, rest 45 seconds
5. Plank shoulder tap: 3 sets of 20 taps, rest 45 seconds

**Progression:** when you hit the top of every rep range for two sessions in a row, make one move harder. Add a plate, slow the tempo or switch to the next variation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an ab workout for men different from one for women?

Not really. Abdominal muscles work the same way for everyone. This routine is written for men looking for gym and home versions, but anyone can follow it and adjust the load and reps.

### Can I do this ab workout every day?

Light core work most days is fine, but the harder versions here work best two or three times a week with a rest day in between, like any other strength training.

### Should I train abs before or after my main lifts?

After. Heavy squats, deadlifts and presses need a fresh core to brace well, so save the ab routine for the end of the session.

### Do I need weights to build abs at home?

No. Slower tempos, pauses, longer holds and harder variations make bodyweight moves challenging. A loaded backpack or water jug is an easy upgrade when you want more resistance.

### How long until I see results?

Most people notice better control and strength within a few weeks of consistent training. Visible definition varies a lot from person to person and depends mostly on body fat, so track what you can measure: reps, hold times and cable weight.
