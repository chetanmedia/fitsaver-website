Hip mobility exercises help when your hips feel stiff after a day at a desk, a long drive or a week of squats and runs. The fix is a short routine done often: a few stretches for the front of the hip, a few for the glutes and inner thighs, and a couple of active drills that teach your hips to use their new range. This guide covers the eight best hip mobility exercises, a 12-minute routine card, and how often to do it for real results.

## Why hips get tight

Most tight hips come from long stretches in one position. When you sit, the muscles at the front of your hip, the hip flexors, stay shortened for hours. [Hospital for Special Surgery physical therapists explain](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/hip-flexor-stretch) that prolonged sitting at a desk, in a car or on a couch keeps the hip flexors compressed, and that runners and cyclists are also prone to tightness because they lift their legs over and over. Weak core and glute muscles can make it worse, because the hip flexors end up doing extra stabilizing work.

It’s a common problem. [Cleveland Clinic notes](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches) that nearly 1 in 4 American adults sit for more than eight hours a day, and that hips can become too tight with inactivity, leading to painful movement and limited flexibility.

### Signs your hips need mobility work

- The front of your hip feels tight or achy after sitting.
- You can’t squat deep without your heels lifting or your back rounding.
- Your stride feels short when you walk or run.
- Sitting cross-legged or getting up from the floor feels harder than it should.

## Mobility vs flexibility

Flexibility is how far a muscle can stretch. Mobility is how well you can actively move a joint through its range. Good hip work includes both: stretches that open up range, and active drills that teach you to control it. That’s why the routine below mixes held stretches with moving drills.

## The 8 best hip mobility exercises

### 1. Kneeling hip flexor stretch

Kneel on one knee with the other foot flat in front. Tuck your pelvis slightly, squeeze the glute of the back leg and shift forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the back hip. Don’t arch your lower back.

- **Targets:** hip flexors.
- **Make it stronger:** reach the same-side arm overhead.

### 2. Couch stretch

Kneel with your back shin up against a couch or wall and your front foot flat. Lift your torso tall. This is a deeper version of the hip flexor stretch that also reaches the front of the thigh.

- **Targets:** hip flexors and quads.
- **Make it easier:** keep your hands on the front knee or a chair for support.

### 3. Pigeon pose

From all fours, bring one knee forward behind your wrist with the shin angled across your body, and slide the other leg back. Stay tall on your hands or fold forward over the front leg.

- **Targets:** glutes and deep hip rotators.
- **Make it easier:** do a lying figure-four stretch on your back instead.

### 4. 90/90 hip switch

Sit with both knees bent at 90 degrees, one leg in front of you and one to the side. Rotate both knees to the other side without using your hands if you can, then switch back. Move slowly.

- **Targets:** internal and external hip rotation.
- **Make it easier:** lean back on your hands.

### 5. Butterfly stretch

Sit with the soles of your feet together and let your knees drop out. Hold your feet and sit tall, then hinge forward slightly for more stretch.

- **Targets:** inner thighs (adductors).
- **Make it easier:** move your feet farther from your body.

### 6. World’s greatest stretch

Step into a deep lunge, place the hand on the same side as the front foot on the floor inside it, then rotate the other arm up toward the ceiling. Return the hand, then step to the other side.

- **Targets:** hip flexors, glutes, inner thighs and upper back.
- **Make it easier:** put your back knee down.

### 7. Deep squat hold (yogi squat)

Sit down into a deep squat with your heels on the floor, chest up and elbows pressing gently against the insides of your knees.

- **Targets:** hips, ankles and lower back.
- **Make it easier:** hold onto a doorframe or put a small book under your heels.

### 8. Glute bridge

Lie on your back, knees bent, and press through your heels to lift your hips. Squeeze your glutes at the top. Strong glutes help the hip flexors relax, which is why strength belongs in a mobility routine. For more glute work, see our [glute workout](/blog/glute-workout-8-best-glute-exercises-for-gym-and-home).

- **Targets:** glutes.
- **Make it stronger:** hold the top for three seconds.

## How long to hold and how often to stretch

HSS physical therapists suggest holding hip flexor stretches for about 30 seconds per side, for three sets, at least twice a day if your hips are very tight. Cleveland Clinic suggests five to eight slow breaths for stretches like the couch stretch, low lunge, figure-four and pigeon pose.

A practical approach for most people:

- **Daily:** a short routine of 10 to 12 minutes, or a few stretches spread through the day.
- **Before workouts:** the active drills (90/90 switches, world’s greatest stretch, bridges) as part of your warm-up.
- **After workouts or in the evening:** the longer holds (couch stretch, pigeon, butterfly).

Consistency matters more than intensity. Stretch to mild tension, never sharp pain. Cleveland Clinic advises seeing a healthcare provider or physical therapist if pain lasts more than a few hours or gets in the way of daily activities.

### Break up long periods of sitting

Stretching helps, but moving more helps too. Stand up, walk around and do a couple of hip flexor stretches every hour or so if you work at a desk. Small breaks add up across a day.

## Hip mobility routine card

Run this once a day, or on rest days and after lower-body workouts. It takes about 12 minutes.

1. **Glute bridge:** 10 reps with a 3-second squeeze at the top, then move on
2. **90/90 hip switch:** 8 slow switches each side, 30 seconds rest
3. **World’s greatest stretch:** 5 reps per side, 30 seconds rest
4. **Kneeling hip flexor stretch:** 2 holds of 30 seconds per side
5. **Pigeon pose:** 2 holds of 30 seconds per side
6. **Butterfly stretch:** 1 hold of 45 seconds
7. **Deep squat hold:** 3 holds of 20 seconds, 15 seconds rest

## Common hip mobility mistakes

- **Arching the lower back in hip flexor stretches.** Tuck your pelvis and squeeze the back glute so the stretch stays in the hip.
- **Forcing the range.** Pushing into pain makes muscles tighten up. Ease in and breathe.
- **Only stretching, never strengthening.** Weak glutes and core can keep the hip flexors overworked.
- **Doing it once a week.** Short, frequent sessions beat a long session you rarely repeat.
- **Bouncing.** Hold stretches steadily or move through drills slowly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best exercise for tight hips?

For hips that feel tight from sitting, the kneeling hip flexor stretch is the most direct option. Pair it with a glute stretch like pigeon pose and an active drill like the 90/90 switch for the best overall result.

### How often should I do hip mobility exercises?

A short routine every day works well, especially if you sit a lot. Even five minutes daily usually beats one long session a week.

### How long does it take to improve hip mobility?

Many people feel looser within a week or two of regular practice. Bigger changes in range of motion take longer and depend on consistency.

### Should I stretch before or after a workout?

Use active drills like 90/90 switches and the world’s greatest stretch before a workout as part of your warm-up. Save longer static holds for after training or a separate session.

### When should I see a professional about hip pain?

See a doctor or physical therapist if you have sharp pain, pain that lasts more than a few hours after stretching, pain that wakes you up, or pain that makes daily activities hard.
