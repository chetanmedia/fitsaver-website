A 5 day workout split for muscle gain works best when it does one thing most popular plans skip: it trains every major muscle at least twice a week. Five sessions give you enough room to do that with real volume, without the marathon workouts of a 3-day plan or the burnout risk of training six days in a row. Below you’ll find the split we recommend (upper, lower, push, pull, legs), the full weekly schedule, a routine card for every day, the sets and reps that drive growth, and a simple way to keep adding weight week after week.

## What is a 5 day workout split?

A 5 day workout split is a weekly plan with five lifting sessions and two rest days, where each session focuses on a different group of muscles. The “split” is how you divide your body across those days.

There are a handful of common ways to divide it:

- **Upper/lower + push/pull/legs (ULPPL):** two days that hit the whole upper or lower body, then three more focused days. Every muscle gets trained twice.
- **Bro split:** one muscle group per day (chest, back, legs, shoulders, arms). Every muscle gets trained once, with a lot of work in that one session.
- **Push/pull/legs + upper/lower in a different order:** the same days as ULPPL, arranged to suit your week.
- **Upper/lower/full body:** alternating upper and lower days with a full body session in the middle.

If you’re still deciding how many days to train at all, our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) compares 3, 4, 5 and 6-day options side by side.

## Which 5 day split is best for muscle gain?

For most people chasing size, the upper/lower + push/pull/legs hybrid is the strongest choice. Here’s why.

### Frequency beats once-a-week marathons

A [systematic review and meta-analysis on training frequency](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) published in *Sports Medicine* found that, when total weekly volume is equal, training a muscle twice a week produced better hypertrophy than training it once a week. The authors concluded that major muscle groups should be trained at least twice a week to maximize growth.

A classic bro split hits each muscle once. You can still grow on it, especially if you enjoy it and show up consistently, but you’re asking one session to do the work of two. By the last few sets of a 20-set chest day, your effort and form usually drop off, so much of that volume is “junk” sets.

### The hybrid spreads the work out

The ULPPL split hits every muscle twice: once on an upper or lower day and once on a push, pull or legs day. Each session stays around 50 to 70 minutes, which is easier to recover from and easier to fit around work. That combination of better frequency and manageable sessions is why we built the plan below around it.

### When a bro split still makes sense

Pick the bro split if you’re an experienced lifter who loves high-volume “pump” sessions, you have flexible time for longer workouts, and you’ve stalled on higher-frequency plans. Beginners usually do better with the hybrid, or with a [4 day workout split](/blog/4-day-workout-split-3-proven-templates-for-every-goal) until training five days feels sustainable.

## The 5 day workout split schedule

Here’s the weekly layout. The two rest days fall after the lower day and after legs, so your legs never get trained on back-to-back days.

| Day | Session | Main focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Upper | Chest, back, shoulders, arms (strength-focused) |
| Tuesday | Lower | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves (strength-focused) |
| Wednesday | Rest | Walk, stretch, recover |
| Thursday | Push | Chest, shoulders, triceps (volume-focused) |
| Friday | Pull | Back, rear delts, biceps (volume-focused) |
| Saturday | Legs | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves (volume-focused) |
| Sunday | Rest | Full rest |

Can’t train Monday to Saturday? Keep the order and slide it. Upper, Lower, rest, Push, Pull, Legs, rest works starting on any day. If you need ideas for what to do on the off days, see our guide to [rest days](/blog/rest-days-what-to-do-and-how-many-you-need).

The first two days use heavier weights and lower reps to build strength. The last three use moderate weights and more reps to pile up volume. Getting stronger in the 6 to 8 rep range makes your 10 to 15 rep sets heavier, and heavier volume is what grows muscle.

## 5 day workout split routine cards

Each card lists sets, reps and rest. Warm up for 5 minutes, then do one or two lighter ramp-up sets of the first exercise before your working sets. Pick a weight that leaves about one or two reps in the tank on most sets.

### Day 1: Upper (strength)

1. **Barbell bench press:** 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, 2 minutes rest
2. **Barbell or chest-supported row:** 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, 2 minutes rest
3. **Seated dumbbell shoulder press:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, 90 seconds rest
4. **Lat pulldown or pull-up:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, 90 seconds rest
5. **EZ-bar curl:** 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
6. **Triceps rope pushdown:** 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest

### Day 2: Lower (strength)

1. **Back squat:** 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, 2 to 3 minutes rest
2. **Romanian deadlift:** 3 sets of 8 reps, 2 minutes rest
3. **Leg press:** 3 sets of 10 reps, 90 seconds rest
4. **Lying leg curl:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
5. **Standing calf raise:** 4 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest

### Day 3: Push (volume)

1. **Incline dumbbell press:** 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, 90 seconds rest
2. **Machine or dumbbell chest press:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 90 seconds rest
3. **Cable chest fly:** 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, 60 seconds rest
4. **Dumbbell lateral raise:** 4 sets of 12 to 15 reps, 45 seconds rest
5. **Overhead cable triceps extension:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest

### Day 4: Pull (volume)

1. **Weighted or assisted pull-up:** 3 sets of 6 to 10 reps, 2 minutes rest
2. **Seated cable row:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 90 seconds rest
3. **Single-arm dumbbell row:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps per side, 60 seconds rest
4. **Face pull:** 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, 45 seconds rest
5. **Incline dumbbell curl:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
6. **Hammer curl:** 2 sets of 12 reps, 60 seconds rest

### Day 5: Legs (volume)

1. **Hack squat or front squat:** 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps, 2 minutes rest
2. **Hip thrust:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 90 seconds rest
3. **Walking lunge:** 3 sets of 10 steps per leg, 90 seconds rest
4. **Seated leg curl:** 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, 60 seconds rest
5. **Leg extension:** 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, 60 seconds rest
6. **Seated calf raise:** 3 sets of 15 reps, 45 seconds rest

## Sets, reps and rest for muscle gain

You don’t need perfect numbers, but you do need enough hard work for each muscle. Use these guidelines to sanity-check the plan or adjust it:

- **Weekly sets per muscle:** roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week. This plan lands most muscles around 10 to 16. Start at the low end and add a set to a lagging muscle only if you’re recovering well.
- **Rep ranges:** muscle grows across a wide range of reps as long as sets are hard. Use 6 to 8 for big lifts, 8 to 12 for most accessories, and 12 to 15 for isolation moves like raises and flies.
- **Effort:** finish most sets with one or two reps left. Going to complete failure on every set adds fatigue faster than it adds muscle.
- **Rest:** 2 to 3 minutes for heavy compound lifts, 60 to 90 seconds for accessories. Resting too little on squats and presses usually costs you reps.
- **Session length:** aim for 50 to 70 minutes. If you’re regularly past 80 minutes, cut a set from each accessory before you cut a day.

For context, the [CDC’s adult activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) recommend muscle-strengthening work on 2 or more days a week that covers all major muscle groups. A 5 day split goes well beyond that baseline, which is why recovery matters so much.

## How to progress on a 5 day split

Muscle gain comes from doing a little more over time. The simplest system is double progression:

1. **Pick a rep range:** for example, 8 to 12 reps on incline dumbbell press.
2. **Work up to the top:** keep the same weight until you hit 12 reps on every set.
3. **Add weight:** move up the smallest jump available, which usually drops you back to 8 or 9 reps.
4. **Repeat:** climb back to 12, then add weight again.

Write down every weight and rep count. Without a log, you’re guessing, and guessing usually means repeating the same weights for months.

Every 6 to 8 weeks, or whenever your lifts stall and joints feel beat up, take a lighter week. Keep the same exercises but cut your sets roughly in half and use about 10% less weight. You’ll usually come back stronger. And keep form honest: [Mayo Clinic’s weight training guide](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/weight-training/art-20045842) puts proper technique first, so slow down, control each rep and use a full range of motion.

## Common 5 day split mistakes

- **Training legs on back-to-back days.** Keep a rest day or an upper day between Lower and Legs. Sore quads ruin a squat session.
- **Adding exercises instead of effort.** Five hard sets beat ten easy ones. If a session feels too easy, push closer to failure before you add more work.
- **Ignoring the rest days.** Muscle is built while you recover. Sleep, enough protein and daily walking count for more than an extra arm session.
- **Changing the plan every week.** Run the same exercises for at least 6 to 8 weeks so you can actually see progress in your log.
- **Skipping the warm-up on heavy days.** A few ramp-up sets before squats and presses cost two minutes and make every working set better.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 5 day split good for building muscle?

Yes. Five days gives you enough sessions to train every major muscle twice a week with plenty of weekly volume, which research on training frequency supports for hypertrophy. The key is choosing a split that hits each muscle twice, like upper/lower + push/pull/legs, rather than once.

### Is a 5 day split good for beginners?

It can be, but many beginners do just as well on three or four days while they learn the lifts. If you’re new and want five days, use this plan with lighter weights, stick to the lower end of the set ranges, and drop to four days if you’re constantly sore or tired.

### How long should each workout be on a 5 day split?

Most sessions in this plan take 50 to 70 minutes, including the warm-up. Heavy days run a little longer because of longer rest periods. If you’re short on time, cut accessory sets rather than whole days.

### Should I do cardio on a 5 day workout split?

Yes, in moderate amounts. Daily walking and two or three short, easy cardio sessions a week support heart health and recovery without eating into your lifting. Keep hard intervals away from the day before Lower or Legs.

### What should I do on the two rest days?

Recover. Walk, stretch, do light mobility work, sleep well and eat enough protein. Avoid turning rest days into extra hard workouts, since that cuts into the recovery your muscles need to grow.
