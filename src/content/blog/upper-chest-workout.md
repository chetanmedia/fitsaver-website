Most chest days start and end with the flat bench, and that’s exactly why so many people feel their chest looks “bottom heavy.” A good chest workout, upper chest first, fixes that by shifting the angle of your presses and flies so the clavicular fibers near your collarbone do more of the work. This guide gives you the six best upper chest exercises, a ready-to-run upper chest workout with sets, reps and rest, and simple rules for fitting it into the week you already train.

## How the upper chest works (and why the angle matters)

Your pectoralis major is one muscle, but its fibers run in different directions. The upper portion, often called the clavicular head, attaches along your collarbone and pulls your upper arm up and across your body. Pressing on a flat bench trains the whole chest, but the middle and lower fibers tend to take the lead. Tilt the bench up and press toward your face, and the upper fibers get a bigger share of the load.

### Why 30 degrees is the sweet spot

Steeper is not better. A [2020 study on five bench inclinations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/) found that upper chest activity peaked at a 30° incline, while angles above 45° shifted more of the work to the front delts and reduced chest performance. In practice, that means setting most adjustable benches to the second or third notch, not the near-vertical setting that turns your press into a shoulder exercise.

- **Low incline (15° to 30°):** the best balance of upper chest work and pressing strength.
- **Moderate incline (around 45°):** still useful, but your shoulders start to take over.
- **Steep incline (60° and up):** closer to an overhead press. Save it for shoulder day.

## The 6 best upper chest exercises

You don’t need a dozen variations. Pick one heavy press, one lighter press, one fly and one finisher, and rotate the rest every few weeks to keep progress moving.

### 1. Incline barbell or Smith machine press

This is your main strength lift. Set the bench to about 30°, grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width and lower it to your upper chest, just below the collarbone. Keep your shoulder blades pulled back and down, feet planted, and press up and slightly back. The Smith machine is a good option if you train alone, because the fixed path and safety catches make it easier to push close to your limit.

### 2. Incline dumbbell press

Dumbbells let each arm move on its own, which evens out side-to-side strength gaps and lets you bring your hands slightly closer together at the top. Lower the weights until you feel a stretch across your upper chest, keep your elbows at roughly 45° from your torso, and press the dumbbells up in a gentle arc.

![Two hands pressing a pair of hex dumbbells upward during an incline dumbbell press](https://framerusercontent.com/images/vhk19faKQvvZcVFJGuOSrl88QyA.jpg)

### 3. Low-to-high cable fly

Set both pulleys near the floor, step forward into a split stance and sweep the handles up and together until they meet around chin height. The upward path lines up with the direction of the upper chest fibers, and the cables keep tension on the muscle the whole way. Use a weight you can control for a full squeeze at the top.

![Man standing between two cable stacks holding the handles out to his sides for a cable chest fly](https://framerusercontent.com/images/Ab698Fj7c1UTe5HNJ3TWbjJWzM.jpg)

### 4. Landmine press

Wedge one end of a barbell into a corner or landmine attachment, hold the other end at shoulder height and press up and forward. The angled path hits the upper chest and is often easier on the shoulders than a barbell bench. Do it kneeling or standing, one arm at a time.

### 5. Feet-elevated push-up

Put your feet on a bench or step and your hands on the floor. Raising your feet turns a regular push-up into an incline press for your upper chest, and it works anywhere. Keep your body in a straight line from heels to head and lower until your chest is just above the floor.

![Man in a gray T-shirt holding the bottom of a push-up on a rubber gym floor](https://framerusercontent.com/images/P9r8a7RsLJpBvAsNyiW8Gppvs.jpg)

### 6. Incline dumbbell fly

On the same 30° bench, hold the dumbbells over your chest with a soft bend in your elbows and open your arms in a wide arc until you feel a stretch. Bring them back up as if you were hugging a barrel. Go lighter than you think; this is a stretch-and-squeeze move, not a strength test.

## Your chest workout: upper chest day, step by step

Run this session once or twice a week. Warm up with 5 minutes of easy cardio and two light sets of the first press before you start counting working sets.

### Upper chest workout routine card

1. **Incline barbell or Smith machine press (30°):** 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, rest 2 minutes
2. **Incline dumbbell press (30°):** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
3. **Low-to-high cable fly:** 3 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 60 seconds
4. **Landmine press:** 3 sets of 10 reps per arm, rest 60 seconds
5. **Feet-elevated push-up:** 2 sets, stop 1 or 2 reps before failure, rest 60 seconds

Total time: about 45 minutes. No cable station? Swap the cable fly for the incline dumbbell fly (3 sets of 12). No barbell? Start with the dumbbell press and add a fourth set.

## How to program an upper chest workout

### How often to train it

For most people, hitting the upper chest directly one or two times a week is plenty, with at least 48 hours between hard chest sessions. That also lines up with the [CDC’s guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) that adults do muscle-strengthening activity at least 2 days a week.

### Where it fits in your split

On a push, pull, legs split, put the incline presses first on push day, before flat pressing or shoulder work. On an upper/lower split, lead one of your upper days with incline work and the other with flat bench. Not sure which split suits your schedule? Our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) walks through 3, 4, 5 and 6-day options.

### Progress a little every week

- Add one rep per set until you reach the top of the rep range, then increase the weight by the smallest jump available.
- Keep form and range of motion the same as the weight goes up, so the progress is real.
- Track every set. Writing down weights is the simplest way to know whether you’re actually getting stronger.

## Form tips for a safer, stronger upper chest

- **Set your shoulder blades first.** Pull them back and down before you unrack, and keep them there.
- **Control the lowering phase.** Take about two seconds on the way down; bouncing the bar shifts work away from the chest.
- **Mind your elbows.** Flaring them straight out to the sides puts extra stress on the front of the shoulder. Aim for roughly 45°.
- **Use a spotter or safeties** on heavy barbell sets, especially when training alone.
- **Stop if something hurts.** A muscle burn is normal; sharp joint pain is a signal to lighten the load or change the exercise.

## Common upper chest workout mistakes

- **Setting the bench too steep.** Past 45°, the front delts take over. Keep most presses around 30°.
- **Only doing flat bench.** Flat pressing is great, but it won’t fully develop the upper chest on its own.
- **Going too heavy on flies.** Heavy flies turn into sloppy presses. Use moderate weight and full control.
- **Skipping the warm-up.** Two light ramp-up sets prepare your shoulders and help you find your groove.
- **Changing the plan every session.** Stick with the same routine for 4 to 6 weeks so you can see progress before swapping exercises.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best exercise for the upper chest?

The incline press, done with a barbell, Smith machine or dumbbells at about 30°, is the most effective single movement because it lets you use heavy weight in the angle that favors the upper fibers. Pair it with a low-to-high cable fly for the best results.

### Can you build your upper chest at home?

Yes. Feet-elevated push-ups, incline dumbbell presses on an adjustable bench and resistance band flies (anchored low and pulled upward) all target the upper chest. Make push-ups harder over time by raising your feet higher or slowing the lowering phase.

### How many sets per week for the upper chest?

A practical starting point is 6 to 10 hard sets of upper chest work per week, spread over one or two sessions. Increase gradually if you recover well and keep progressing.

### Should I train upper chest before or after flat bench?

If upper chest is your priority, train it first, while you’re fresh. You can still do flat pressing afterward with slightly less weight, or alternate which one leads from week to week.
