A good apartment workout has one extra rule that a gym session doesn’t: the person living under you shouldn’t be able to follow along. That rules out burpees, jump squats, skipping and anything else that sends your body weight into the floor. The good news is that you don’t need any of it. Slow, controlled bodyweight strength work builds muscle and gets your heart rate up, and done right it makes about as much noise as folding laundry. This guide explains why some moves are loud and others aren’t, gives you eight quiet exercises, and ends with a 25-minute no-jump routine card you can run three times a week.

## Why apartment workouts get loud (and how to fix it)

Most workout noise in an apartment isn’t the sound you hear. It’s impact noise: the thud that travels through the floor structure and comes out of your neighbor’s ceiling. Music and voices travel through the air and fade fast. A heel strike or a landing travels through the building itself, and it carries.

That gives you a simple test for any exercise: **does your body ever leave the floor, or does anything land on it?** If the answer is no, the move is apartment-friendly. If the answer is yes, swap it for a slower version.

Three habits take care of most of the remaining noise:

- **Keep one point of contact.** Step instead of hop. A reverse lunge does the same job as a jumping lunge for your legs with none of the landing.
- **Slow the lowering phase.** Taking two or three seconds to lower into a squat or push-up removes the slap at the bottom and makes the rep harder, which is the point.
- **Train on a cushioned surface.** A thick exercise mat over a rug absorbs most of what’s left. Bare feet or soft-soled shoes are quieter than sneakers with hard heels.

### Timing matters too

Even quiet sessions sound louder at 6 a.m. If you share floors and ceilings, aim for daytime or early evening, and save anything that involves dragging furniture or setting weights down for those hours. Many buildings list quiet hours in the lease, so it’s worth a quick look.

## How to set up a quiet workout space

You need a space roughly the size of a yoga mat plus an arm’s length on each side. That’s it.

![A pair of dumbbells and a black exercise mat set up on a rug at home](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1683758507025-6e74ad3ca1e5?crop=entropy&cs=srgb&fm=jpg&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1600&auto=format)

- **Mat:** A 6 to 10 mm exercise mat is the single best noise investment. If you have a rug, put the mat on top of it.
- **Placement:** Train over the middle of a room or over a rug rather than right next to a shared wall, where furniture vibrations travel more easily.
- **Optional gear:** A resistance band or a pair of light dumbbells adds challenge without adding noise, as long as you put weights down on the mat rather than on bare floor. If you’re deciding what to buy, our [home workout equipment guide](/blog/home-workout-equipment) covers the options.
- **Your phone or a screen:** Prop it where you can see the timer without walking over to it mid-set.

## The 8 best quiet apartment workout exercises

Each move below keeps at least one foot or hand on the floor at all times. Together they cover legs, glutes, chest, back, shoulders and core, which is everything a full-body session needs.

### 1. Tempo bodyweight squat

Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart. Take three full seconds to sit down and back, pause for one second at the bottom, then stand up at normal speed.

- **Cue:** Push your knees out in line with your toes and keep your heels down.
- **Why it’s quiet:** No bounce at the bottom and no jump at the top. The slow lowering is what makes it hard.

### 2. Reverse lunge

From standing, step one foot back and lower until both knees are bent to about 90 degrees, then drive through the front foot to stand. Set the back foot down softly, toe first.

- **Cue:** Keep most of your weight in the front heel.
- **Why it’s quiet:** Stepping back is easier to control than stepping forward, so the back foot lands gently instead of stamping.

### 3. Glute bridge

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Press through your heels to lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees, squeeze for two seconds, then lower slowly.

- **Progression:** Do it on one leg, or rest a dumbbell across your hips.
- **Why it’s quiet:** You’re lying down the whole time. This is one of the quietest strength moves there is.

### 4. Push-up (or incline push-up)

Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in one straight line. Lower your chest toward the floor over two to three seconds, then press back up. If full push-ups are too hard for the reps on the card, put your hands on a couch arm or a sturdy table.

- **Cue:** Elbows at roughly 45 degrees from your body, not flared straight out.
- **Why it’s quiet:** Lowering under control means your chest never drops onto the mat.

### 5. Lateral lunge

Stand with feet together. Take a wide step to one side, sit your hips back over that foot while the other leg stays straight, then push back to the start. Alternate sides.

- **Cue:** Keep your chest up and both feet pointing forward.
- **Why it’s useful:** Most home workouts only move forward and back. Side-to-side work hits your inner thighs and hips and makes the session more complete.

### 6. Superman row

Lie face down with arms stretched overhead. Lift your chest and arms a few inches, pull your elbows down toward your ribs as if doing a row, extend again, then lower. This works the upper back, which push-ups and squats mostly miss.

- **Cue:** Look at the floor so your neck stays long.
- **Band option:** Loop a resistance band around a closed door handle and do standing rows instead.

### 7. Forearm plank

Elbows under shoulders, body straight from head to heels, belly tight. Hold. [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/want-a-stronger-core-skip-the-sit-ups) points to planks as a better core exercise than sit-ups because they train the core to hold the spine steady rather than flexing it over and over.

- **Cue:** Squeeze your glutes and pull your elbows toward your toes without moving them.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips sag. Drop to your knees before your form goes.

### 8. Slow mountain climber

Start in a high plank. Draw one knee toward your chest, place that foot back down softly, then switch. It’s the quiet version of the fast, bouncy climber you see in most HIIT videos.

- **Cue:** Keep your hips level with your shoulders.
- **Make it cardio:** Pick up the pace only as much as you can while keeping every foot placement silent.

## How to make a no-jump workout harder

The common worry with quiet workouts is that they get too easy. They only do if you keep doing the same thing. Instead of adding jumps, add one of these:

### Slow it down

Tempo is your main tool. A squat with a three-second lowering phase and a one-second pause is far harder than ten fast ones. When a move feels easy, add a second to the way down.

### Go one-sided

Single-leg glute bridges, split squats (a reverse lunge where you stay in the bottom position and pump up and down) and single-arm variations double the load on one side without any equipment.

### Add more reps or rounds, then load

Add a few reps each week. Once you can do every set on the card with clean form, add a fourth round, or add a band or a light dumbbell. The [NHS strength exercise guide](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/) uses the same idea: start with bodyweight moves you can do at home and progress gradually.

### Shorten rest, not form

Cutting rest from 45 seconds to 30 turns the routine into quiet cardio. Just don’t let speed turn controlled steps into stomps.

## How often to do this apartment workout

For most people, three sessions a week on non-consecutive days is a strong starting point, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. That lines up with the [CDC’s adult guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html), which recommend muscle-strengthening activity that works all major muscle groups at least two days a week, alongside about 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity such as brisk walking.

On the other days, go for a walk outside, do some light stretching, or take a full rest day. ## Quiet apartment workout routine card

Do three rounds. Rest 45 seconds between exercises and 90 seconds between rounds. The full session takes about 25 minutes, including a short warm-up of marching in place, arm circles and hip circles.

1. **Tempo bodyweight squat:** 12 reps, 3 seconds down and 1 second pause, 45 seconds rest
2. **Reverse lunge:** 10 reps per leg, 45 seconds rest
3. **Push-up or incline push-up:** 8 to 12 reps, 2 to 3 seconds down, 45 seconds rest
4. **Glute bridge:** 15 reps with a 2-second squeeze at the top, 45 seconds rest
5. **Lateral lunge:** 8 reps per side, 45 seconds rest
6. **Superman row:** 10 reps, 45 seconds rest
7. **Forearm plank:** 30 to 45 seconds hold, 45 seconds rest
8. **Slow mountain climber:** 20 total steps, 90 seconds rest before the next round

## Frequently asked questions

### What exercises can I do in an apartment without making noise?

Any move where your body never leaves the floor: squats, reverse and lateral lunges, glute bridges, push-ups, planks, superman rows, slow mountain climbers and most stretching or mobility work. Avoid jumps, burpees, skipping and fast high knees.

### Can you get a good workout without jumping?

Yes. Strength and muscle come from working hard against resistance, not from leaving the ground. Slowing the tempo, going single-leg and cutting rest periods make no-jump moves challenging enough for most people, and short rest turns the circuit into cardio as well.

### How do I stop my workout from bothering my downstairs neighbor?

Train on a thick mat over a rug, step instead of hop, lower every rep slowly, put weights down on the mat, and work out at reasonable hours. Those five habits remove nearly all impact noise.

### Is a 25-minute apartment workout enough?

For general fitness, yes, especially done three times a week. The routine card trains every major muscle group, which meets the strength part of the CDC’s guidelines. Add walks or other moderate activity on the other days to cover the rest.

### Do I need any equipment for an apartment workout?

No. Everything on the routine card uses bodyweight only. A good mat is the one thing worth buying for comfort and noise, and a resistance band is a cheap, quiet way to add challenge later.
