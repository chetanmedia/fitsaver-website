A hotel room workout is the easiest way to keep your training on track when you’re away from home. You don’t need a gym, a booking or any gear. You need a patch of floor about the size of a yoga mat, 20 minutes and a plan you decided on before you got tired from travel. This guide gives you that plan: a 20-minute, no-equipment routine that works your legs, chest, back and core, keeps the noise down for the neighbors, and fits between a late check-in and an early meeting.

## Why a hotel room workout works better than you think

Most people skip workouts on the road for one reason: they never decided what they would do. The hotel gym is closed, too small or packed, so the default becomes “I’ll start again when I’m home.” A short routine you can do next to the bed removes that excuse. It also keeps your week from turning into a full reset when you get back.

You don’t have to match your normal training volume while traveling. The goal is to keep the habit alive and give your muscles a reason to stay ready. The [CDC’s physical activity guidelines for adults](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus muscle-strengthening work on two or more days. Two or three 20-minute hotel sessions on a business trip get you a long way toward the strength part of that.

### What you need (almost nothing)

- A clear space roughly 6 feet by 3 feet, usually between the bed and the desk or by the window
- A towel folded in half for your knees or back on hard floors
- A sturdy desk, bed edge or wall for incline push-ups and balance
- Your phone with a timer, or a workout app that runs rest timers for you
- Water, because hotel air is often dry

### Keep it quiet

Hotel floors and walls are thin, and nobody wants to be the guest doing jump squats at 6 a.m. Every move in this routine is a **no-jump** version. You can still raise the effort by slowing down the lowering phase, adding a pause at the bottom or squeezing in a few more reps per interval.

## The 20-minute hotel room workout routine (no equipment)

This hotel room workout routine runs in three parts: a 3-minute warm-up, three rounds of a 5-move circuit, and a 2-minute cool-down. Each circuit move is 40 seconds of work followed by 20 seconds of rest, so one round takes 5 minutes and three rounds take 15. If you’ve only got 10 minutes, do the warm-up and one or two rounds. If you want more, add a fourth round.

### Warm-up (3 minutes)

- **Marching in place with arm swings**: 60 seconds
- **Hip circles and torso twists**: 60 seconds
- **Slow bodyweight squats to a comfortable depth**: 60 seconds

### 1. Bodyweight squat

Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart, toes turned slightly out. Push your hips back and bend your knees as if sitting into a chair, keep your chest up, then drive through your whole foot to stand. For more challenge, take three seconds to lower and pause for one second at the bottom.

### 2. Incline or floor push-up

Place your hands on the edge of a sturdy desk or the bed frame (not the soft mattress) and walk your feet back until your body is a straight line. Lower your chest toward the edge, then press away. The higher the surface, the easier it is. Move to the floor, or to your knees on a folded towel, when you want to adjust the difficulty.

### 3. Reverse lunge

From standing, step one foot back and lower until both knees are bent around 90 degrees, then push through the front heel to return. Alternate legs. Reverse lunges are easier on the knees than forward lunges and need less space, which matters in a narrow room. Hold the desk or wall lightly if your balance is off after a flight.

### 4. Glute bridge

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, hip-width apart. Press through your heels to lift your hips until your body forms a line from shoulders to knees, squeeze your glutes at the top, then lower with control. For a harder version, do single-leg bridges and switch legs halfway through the interval.

### 5. Plank with shoulder taps

Set up in a high plank with hands under shoulders and feet a little wider than hip-width. Slowly tap one hand to the opposite shoulder, then switch, keeping your hips as still as you can. If that’s too much, hold a regular forearm plank. [Harvard Health describes planks](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/want-a-stronger-core-skip-the-sit-ups) as one of the best ways to train your core, and they need no space at all.

### Cool-down (2 minutes)

- **Standing quad stretch**: 30 seconds each leg, holding the wall for balance
- **Child’s pose or a seated forward fold**: 30 seconds
- **Chest opener with hands clasped behind your back**: 30 seconds

## How to make your hotel room workout harder or easier

Your energy on a trip changes a lot from day to day. Jet lag, long meetings and restaurant meals all play a part. Use these adjustments instead of skipping the session.

### On a low-energy day

- Do one or two rounds instead of three
- Switch to 30 seconds of work and 30 seconds of rest
- Use the highest incline for push-ups and a shallower squat
- Hold a forearm plank instead of shoulder taps

### When you want more

- Add a fourth round, which brings the session to about 25 minutes
- Use a 3-second lowering phase on every rep
- Swap squats for split squats with your back foot on the floor and add a pause
- Do floor push-ups with your feet on the bed frame for a decline version

### If the room is tiny

Swap reverse lunges for **split squats** (stay in the lunge stance and move up and down without stepping), and do everything in the space a towel covers. A hotel room workout with no equipment should never need more room than that.

## Plan your travel workouts before you pack

The best hotel workouts are decided at home. A few minutes of planning means you open your phone, press start and go, instead of searching for ideas when you’re already tired.

1. **Pick your training days.** On a 4-day trip, choose two or three mornings or evenings and treat them like meetings.
2. **Choose one main routine.** The circuit above is enough. Repeating it lets you track progress, such as more reps in the same 40 seconds.
3. **Keep one short backup.** A 10-minute version (warm-up plus one round) is for the day everything runs late.
4. **Pack for the floor.** Comfortable shorts or leggings and a light shirt take almost no space. Sneakers are optional for this routine.
5. **Get back on schedule when you’re home.** If you follow a weekly plan, our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) helps you slot the trip into it without losing a full week.

Here is the full session in one place. Screenshot it, or set it up in your workout app before you travel.

7. Cool-down: quad stretch, child’s pose and chest opener, 2 minutes total

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 20-minute hotel room workout enough?

For maintaining strength and the habit on a short trip, yes. Twenty focused minutes that train your whole body two or three times a week is much better than nothing, and it makes getting back to your normal plan easier.

### Can I build muscle with a no-equipment hotel workout?

You can keep the muscle you have and still make progress, especially if you’re newer to training. Make bodyweight moves harder with slower reps, pauses, single-leg variations and more rounds.

### How do I work out in a hotel room without disturbing anyone?

Skip jumping moves, work on a folded towel and control every rep on the way down. Everything in this routine is low-impact and quiet enough for early mornings.

### Should I do the hotel room workout in the morning or evening?

Pick the time you can protect. Mornings are often easier on trips because evenings fill up with dinners and calls. If you arrive late, a 10-minute version before bed still counts.
