A cable chest workout fixes the two things free weights do badly: it keeps tension on your pecs through the whole rep, and it lets you change the angle of pull just by moving a pulley up or down. That makes cables one of the easiest ways to hit your upper, middle and lower chest in a single session without switching between benches, racks and dumbbell rows. Below: how pulley height changes the exercise, six cable chest exercises worth your time, a routine card, and simple rules for progressing.

## Why a cable chest workout works

Your chest’s main job is bringing your upper arm across your body, with bent elbows (presses) or nearly straight arms (flies). Cables do both well, with three advantages free weights can’t match.

- **Constant tension.** With a dumbbell fly, the load almost disappears at the top because gravity pulls straight down. A cable pulls along its own line, so your chest is still working when your hands meet.
- **Any angle you want.** Moving the pulley from high to low shifts the work from the lower fibers of your chest toward the upper fibers near your collarbone. No adjustable bench needed.
- **Small, safe jumps in load.** Stacks move in small plates, and a failed rep just returns the handles to the machine.

Cables also hold up well against the classics. In an [ACE-sponsored study of nine common chest exercises](https://www.acefitness.org/about-ace/press-room/press-releases/2930/ace-study-tests-common-chest-exercises-finds-barbell-bench-press-most-effective/), the barbell bench press, the pec deck and bent-forward cable crossovers produced the most chest muscle activation, with the cable crossover reaching 93 percent of the bench press. You don’t need to choose between them: a press for heavy work plus a cable fly for tension is a strong pairing.

## Pulley height: the cable chest workout cheat sheet

The angle of the cable decides which part of your chest does the most work. Think of it like this: your arms should move along the same line as the cable.

| Pulley height | Direction of your hands | Chest region emphasized | Example exercise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High (above head) | Down and in, toward your hips | Lower chest | High-to-low cable fly |
| Middle (chest height) | Straight forward and in | Middle chest | Standing cable chest press, mid cable fly |
| Low (near the floor) | Up and in, toward your chin | Upper chest | Low-to-high cable fly |

Your chest is one muscle, so every variation trains all of it to some degree. The angle just shifts the emphasis. If upper chest is your priority, our [upper chest workout](/blog/upper-chest-workout) goes deeper on incline angles.

### Stance and setup

Most cable chest exercises are done standing in the middle of a dual cable station.

- **Staggered stance.** Step one foot forward. It stops the stack from pulling you backward and gives your lower back a stable base.
- **Slight forward lean.** Hinge a little at the hips so your chest sits between the two cables.
- **Ribs down, shoulders back.** Keep your shoulder blades gently pulled back so your shoulders don’t roll forward at the end of each rep.
- **Soft elbows on flies.** Keep a slight, fixed bend. If your elbows open and close, the fly turns into a press.

## The 6 best cable chest exercises

Each exercise includes the setup, a key cue and a common mistake. Start light: pulley ratios differ between machines, so the numbers on the stack don’t translate.

### 1. Standing cable chest press

Set both pulleys around chest height and grab a D-handle in each hand. Step forward into a staggered stance with the handles beside your chest, then press forward until your arms are straight and your hands are close together.

- **Cue:** Press “forward and slightly in,” finishing with your hands in front of your sternum.
- **Common mistake:** Leaning so far forward that your body weight does the pressing. Stay tall enough that your chest and arms move the load.

### 2. Mid cable fly

Keep the pulleys at chest height. Start with your arms open wide, elbows slightly bent, palms facing forward. Bring your hands together in a wide hugging arc until they meet in front of your chest, squeeze for a second, then let them open slowly until you feel a stretch across your chest.

- **Cue:** Imagine wrapping your arms around a large tree trunk.
- **Common mistake:** Going too heavy. If your shoulders shrug or your elbows bend more on each rep, drop the weight.

### 3. Low-to-high cable fly

Set the pulleys at the lowest position. With palms facing up and a slight bend in your elbows, sweep your hands up and in until they meet around chin or upper-chest height. This is the go-to cable exercise for the upper chest.

- **Cue:** Lead with your pinkies and finish with your hands at eye level or just below, never overhead.
- **Common mistake:** Turning it into a front raise. Keep the motion an arc toward the middle, not straight up.

### 4. High-to-low cable fly (cable crossover)

Set the pulleys at the top. Lean forward slightly from your hips and bring your hands down and across until they meet in front of your hips or lower stomach. This is the bent-forward cable crossover from the ACE study, and it’s a great finisher for the lower chest.

- **Cue:** Think “hands to belt buckle.” You can cross one hand over the other at the bottom for a stronger squeeze.
- **Common mistake:** Pulling with your arms and lats. Keep the elbow bend fixed and move from the shoulder.

### 5. Single-arm cable fly

Set one pulley at chest height and stand side-on to it. Bring one hand across your body to the midline, then return slowly. Working one side at a time lets you correct a weaker side and adds a core challenge, because your torso has to resist rotating.

- **Cue:** Keep your hips and shoulders square to the front. Only the working arm moves.
- **Common mistake:** Twisting your torso to finish the rep. Shorten the range or lighten the load instead.

### 6. Incline bench cable fly or press

Place an incline bench between the cable towers with the pulleys set low. Lie back and do a fly or press from there. The incline plus the low pulley puts the cables in line with your upper chest, and the bench takes balance out of the equation so you can focus on your pecs.

- **Cue:** Keep your shoulder blades pinned to the bench the whole time.
- **Home option:** Anchor two resistance bands low behind you and press or fly from a sturdy incline or a floor bridge.

## How to program a cable chest workout

You can run a full cable chest workout on its own, or use two or three of these exercises after a barbell or dumbbell press. Either way, the basic rules are the same.

### Picking a starting weight

Start with a weight you can move with clean form for 12 to 15 reps. [Mayo Clinic’s weight training guidance](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/weight-training/art-20045842) recommends exactly that range for choosing a starting load, along with moving the weight in a controlled way and warming up first with 5 to 10 minutes of brisk walking or other light cardio. Note the pin number for each exercise on day one.

### Sets, reps and rest

- **Presses:** 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, 60 to 90 seconds rest.
- **Flies:** 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps, 45 to 60 seconds rest.
- **Tempo:** Take two to three seconds to let the handles return. The stretch at the start of each fly is where a lot of the work happens.

### How often to train chest

Two chest sessions a week, with at least one day between them, works well for most people. That fits the [CDC’s adult activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html), which call for muscle-strengthening work on at least two days a week covering all major muscle groups, including the chest. If you follow a push-pull-legs or upper-lower plan, cable chest work slots straight into your push or upper days. Our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) shows where it fits in a 3, 4, 5 or 6-day week.

### Progression

When you hit the top of the rep range on every set with good form, add load. If the next pin up is too big a jump, add one or two reps per set first, or slow the return to four seconds. Small, steady increases beat big jumps that pull your form apart.

## Cable chest workout routine card

Here’s a complete cable chest workout that hits all three regions. It takes about 35 to 45 minutes, including a short warm-up. Run it once or twice a week.

1. **Standing cable chest press:** 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps, 90 seconds rest
2. **Low-to-high cable fly:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
3. **Incline bench cable press:** 3 sets of 10 reps, 75 seconds rest
4. **Mid cable fly:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 60 seconds rest
5. **Single-arm cable fly:** 2 sets of 12 reps per side, 45 seconds rest
6. **High-to-low cable fly:** 3 sets of 15 reps, 45 seconds rest

Warm up with a few minutes of light cardio and one easy set of the first exercise at about half your working weight. Stop any movement that causes sharp or pinching pain in your shoulder.

## Common cable chest workout mistakes

- **Standing too close to the stack.** If the plates touch down at the start of each rep, you lose tension. Step forward until the weight stays lifted.
- **Letting the weight yank you open.** Control the return. A fast, bouncy stretch strains the front of the shoulder.
- **Bending and straightening your elbows on flies.** That turns the fly into a press and takes work off your chest.
- **Shrugging.** Shoulders up by your ears means the load is too heavy or your shoulder blades aren’t set.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you build a big chest with only cables?

Yes, especially as a beginner or intermediate lifter. Cables let you load your chest at every angle and progress steadily. Many people still keep one press, like the bench press or a dumbbell press, because it allows heavier loading, then use cables for the rest of the session.

### Are cable flies better than dumbbell flies?

They’re different. Dumbbell flies load the chest most at the bottom stretch and very little at the top. Cable flies keep tension through the whole range, including the squeeze where your hands meet. Both work; cables are usually easier to progress and gentler at the bottom.

### What cable height is best for the upper chest?

A low pulley. Low-to-high flies and incline bench cable presses put the line of pull in line with the upper fibers of your chest near the collarbone.

### How many cable chest exercises should I do in one workout?

Three to five is plenty: one press, one upper chest fly and one lower or mid fly. Trim the six-move card to three or four if you’re training chest after another press.

### How often should I do a cable chest workout?

Once or twice a week is enough for most people. Leave at least one day between chest sessions so the muscle can recover.
