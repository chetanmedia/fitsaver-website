Full body workout vs split: it’s one of the first questions people ask once they’ve saved a pile of workout videos and want to turn them into a real plan. A full body workout trains every major muscle group in each session. A split divides your body into parts, like upper and lower or push, pull and legs, and trains each part on its own day. Both build strength and muscle. The better choice is the one that fits your schedule, your experience and the number of days you can reliably show up. This guide compares the two, gives you a quick decision table, and finishes with ready-to-use routines for both styles.

## What is a full body workout?

A full body workout hits your legs, your push muscles (chest, shoulders and triceps), your pull muscles (back and biceps) and your core in one session. Most full body plans run two to four days a week with at least one rest day between sessions. Each workout usually has five to seven exercises built around big compound lifts like squats, presses, rows and hinges.

![Woman doing a dumbbell goblet squat in a bright gym, a core move in many full body workouts](https://framerusercontent.com/images/DE2jpKZ2XHBWKLGJK3gEthDxJoE.jpg)

- **Typical frequency:** 2 to 4 sessions a week
- **Session length:** about 45 to 70 minutes
- **How often each muscle is trained:** 2 to 3 times a week
- **Classic format:** an A/B plan that alternates two different full body workouts

## What is a workout split?

A split divides training by body part or movement pattern. Instead of doing everything every session, you focus on a few muscle groups per day and spread the work across the week. For a deeper look at every option, our [workout split guide](/blog/workout-split-guide-how-to-pick-the-right-3-4-5-or-6-day-split) covers 3, 4, 5 and 6-day setups.

### Common split types

- **Upper/lower:** upper body one day, lower body the next, usually 4 days a week
- **Push/pull/legs (PPL):** chest, shoulders and triceps; back and biceps; legs. Run it once (3 days) or twice (6 days) a week
- **Body part split:** one or two muscle groups per day, often 5 days a week
- **Full body workout split hybrid:** two full body days plus one upper or lower day

## Full body workout vs split: the key differences

When people compare a full body workout vs split workout, they’re really comparing four things: how often each muscle gets trained, how much total work you do, how you recover, and how much your week can flex.

### Training frequency

Full body plans train each muscle more often, while a classic body part split might hit a muscle only once a week. A 2016 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine found that [training each muscle group twice a week](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8) produced better muscle growth than once a week when total volume was matched. Full body plans get you there by default, and so do upper/lower or PPL run twice a week.

### Total weekly volume

Volume means the total hard sets you do for a muscle each week. A later 2019 review of 25 studies concluded that when [weekly volume is equal, training frequency does not meaningfully change muscle growth](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906), so you can choose a frequency based on personal preference. In plain terms: get your weekly sets in, and how you spread them across the week is up to you.

### Recovery and fatigue

Full body sessions spread effort across your whole body, so no single muscle gets crushed. Splits let you work one area hard, then give it several days off. If you love long, high-volume days for one muscle group, a split suits you. If you prefer moderate sessions and feeling fresh most days, full body is the easier fit.

### Schedule flexibility

This is where full body plans shine. Miss a day on a body part split and a whole muscle group may go untrained that week. Miss a full body day and you still trained everything in your last session. For busy weeks, travel or shift work, that forgiveness matters more than any small difference in results.

## When a full body workout is the better choice

- You’re new to lifting and want to practice the main movements often
- You can only train 2 or 3 days a week
- Your schedule changes from week to week
- You also run, play a sport or take classes and need room to recover
- You want short, repeatable plans you can do anywhere, like this [hotel room workout](/blog/hotel-room-workout-a-20-minute-no-equipment-routine) for travel days

## When a split is the better choice

- You can train 4 or more days a week consistently
- You’ve been lifting for a year or more and full body sessions now feel too long
- You want extra sets for a lagging body part
- You enjoy focusing on one area per session
- Most of the workout videos you save are split-style days, like chest day or leg day

Both approaches clear that bar easily.

## Routine card: 3-day full body workout (A/B)

Alternate Workout A and Workout B on three non-consecutive days, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Week 1 is A, B, A. Week 2 is B, A, B. Choose a weight that leaves one or two reps in reserve on your last set, and add a little weight or a rep when every set feels solid.

![Man doing a one-arm dumbbell row on a bench, part of a full body workout routine](https://framerusercontent.com/images/A4LIQ9fQ1cQIDN73jjDOBFeZoak.jpg)

### Workout A

1. **Goblet squat or back squat:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
2. **Dumbbell bench press:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
3. **One-arm dumbbell row:** 3 sets of 10 reps per side, rest 60 seconds
4. **Romanian deadlift:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
5. **Dumbbell shoulder press:** 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, rest 60 seconds
6. **Plank:** 3 holds of 30 to 45 seconds, rest 45 seconds

### Workout B

1. **Deadlift or trap bar deadlift:** 3 sets of 5 to 6 reps, rest 2 minutes
2. **Incline dumbbell press:** 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
3. **Lat pulldown or assisted pull-up:** 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps, rest 90 seconds
4. **Reverse lunge:** 3 sets of 10 reps per leg, rest 60 seconds
5. **Face pull or band pull-apart:** 2 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 45 seconds
6. **Dead bug:** 3 sets of 8 reps per side, rest 45 seconds

### Split alternative: 4-day upper/lower

Have four days? Run the same movements as an upper/lower split. Do Upper on Monday and Thursday and Lower on Tuesday and Friday, using 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps with 60 to 90 seconds of rest.

- **Upper:** bench press, dumbbell row, shoulder press, lat pulldown, biceps curl, triceps pushdown
- **Lower:** squat, Romanian deadlift, reverse lunge, leg curl, calf raise, plank

## How to switch between full body and a split

You don’t have to choose forever. Many people run full body for a few months, move to a split when sessions start to drag, and go back to full body during busy seasons. A few rules keep the switch smooth:

1. Keep your main lifts the same so you can compare progress
2. Match your weekly sets per muscle as closely as you can
3. Take a slightly lighter first week on the new plan
4. Log your weights every session so you can see you’re still progressing
5. Give any plan at least 6 to 8 weeks before judging it

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a full body workout better than a split for beginners?

For most beginners, yes. Practicing squats, presses, rows and hinges two or three times a week helps you learn technique faster, and missing a session costs you less.

### Can you build muscle with full body workouts only?

Yes. Muscle growth depends mainly on hard sets, gradual progression, food and sleep. A full body plan can deliver plenty of weekly volume as long as you push each set close to failure and add weight or reps over time.

### What is a full body workout split?

It usually means a weekly plan built from full body sessions, such as three full body days, or a hybrid that mixes two full body days with an upper or lower day. It keeps frequency high while adding some focus.

### How many days a week should I train with a split?

Most splits need four to six days to train every muscle twice a week. If you can only commit to three days, a full body plan is usually the better fit.

### Can I do a full body workout every day?

It isn’t necessary, and most people recover better with at least one day off between hard full body sessions. Light activity like walking or mobility work on off days is fine.
