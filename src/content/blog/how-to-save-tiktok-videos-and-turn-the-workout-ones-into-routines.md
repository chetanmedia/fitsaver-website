Knowing how to save TikTok videos is easy once you know where TikTok hides each option. There are really three different “saves”: a bookmark inside the app (Favorites), a download to your phone’s camera roll, and a way to keep your own clips without posting them publicly. This guide walks through all three, step by step, then tackles the part nobody else covers: what to do with the dozens of workout videos you’ve saved so they turn into training sessions instead of a graveyard of good intentions.

## How to save TikTok videos: the three options at a glance

Before you tap anything, decide what you actually want the save to do. Each option solves a different problem.

- **Favorites (bookmark):** Keeps the video inside TikTok, linked to the original post. Best for workouts, recipes and tutorials you want to come back to. It costs no phone storage, but the video can disappear if the creator deletes it.
- **Save video (download):** Puts a copy in your camera roll or gallery. Best when you need the clip offline or want to send it to someone outside TikTok. It only works when the creator allows downloads.
- **Save your own video without posting:** Keeps a clip you made yourself, either as a private post or as a file on your phone. Best for drafts, practice takes and form checks.

Menu labels move a little between app updates and between iPhone and Android, but the locations below have stayed consistent for a long time.

## How to add a TikTok to Favorites

Favorites is the fastest way to save a video, and it’s the one most people mean when they say “I saved it.”

1. Open the video in your For You or Following feed.
2. Tap the **bookmark icon** on the right side of the screen, under the comment icon. It turns yellow and a small “Added to Favorites” message appears.
3. To find it later, go to **Profile**, then tap the **Favorites** tab (the bookmark icon above your grid).

You can also press and hold on a video and choose **Add to Favorites** from the menu that pops up.

### Organize Favorites into collections

Favorites become a mess fast, so set up collections early.

1. Go to **Profile > Favorites > Collections**.
2. Tap **Create new collection** and give it a clear name, such as “Leg day” or “Core, 10 minutes.”
3. Select the videos you want and tap **Add videos**.

Collections are private unless you switch on the option to make them public. Name them by what you’ll do, not by who posted them. “Upper body, dumbbells” is easier to act on than “Sam’s videos.”

## How to save TikTok videos to your camera roll

Downloading makes a real copy on your phone. According to [TikTok’s help page on downloading videos](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/video-downloads), you can save someone else’s video only when the creator allows it.

1. Open the video.
2. Tap the **Share** arrow on the right side.
3. Tap **Save video** (sometimes shown as **Download**) in the bottom row.
4. Wait for the progress circle to finish. The video lands in your Photos app on iPhone or your Gallery on Android.

If **Save video** isn’t in the share menu, the creator has turned downloads off. TikTok says plainly that a missing save option means the creator doesn’t allow it. In that case, use Favorites instead, or copy the link (Share > Copy link) so you can open the original later.

### Why the saved file has a watermark

Downloads from TikTok usually include a moving watermark with the TikTok logo and the creator’s username. That’s by design: it keeps credit attached when clips get shared around. Third-party “no watermark” download sites exist, but they sit in a gray area, often fill pages with aggressive ads, and strip the creator’s credit. If you just want to follow the workout, you don’t need a watermark-free file at all.

## How to save TikTok videos without posting

This is the most searched version of the question, and it applies to your own clips: a form check, a practice run of a routine, or a draft you’re not ready to share. TikTok drafts live only inside the app, so if you delete the app or switch phones, drafts can be lost. Here are two reliable ways to keep a copy.

### Method 1: Post it as “Only me”

1. Record or upload your video and tap **Next**.
2. On the post screen, tap **Who can watch this video** (or **Privacy**) and choose **Only me**.
3. Tap **Post**. Nobody else can see it, and it sits behind the lock icon tab on your profile.
4. To get a file, open the private video, tap the **three dots** or **Share** button, and choose **Save video**.

### Method 2: Save to device while you post

On the same post screen, open **More options** and switch on **Save to device** (on some versions it’s called **Save posts** or **Save to camera roll**). Post the video as **Only me**, and TikTok saves a copy to your camera roll at the same time. This route is handy if you want the file without hunting for it afterward.

Either way, you can delete the private post later. The copy in your camera roll stays.

### How to save TikTok videos to camera roll without the watermark (your own clips)

For your own recordings, the simplest fix is to film with your phone’s regular camera first, then upload that file to TikTok. The original in your camera roll never had a watermark. For clips you already recorded inside TikTok, the private-post download will carry your username, which is fine for personal use.

## The real problem: a Favorites tab full of workouts you never do

Here’s what happens to most fitness saves. You tap the bookmark on a 30-second leg workout, then a core finisher, then a mobility flow. A month later there are 140 videos in Favorites, no names, no order, and when you’re standing in the gym you scroll for five minutes, give up, and do the same three machines as always.

Saving feels like progress, but a saved video isn’t a plan. It’s missing the things that make a session runnable:

- **The exercise list.** Many fitness videos flash each move for a second or two. You have to rewatch to catch the names.
- **Sets, reps and rest.** Creators often show one round or skip the numbers entirely.
- **A place in your week.** A video sitting in a list doesn’t tell you when you’ll do it.
- **A record.** You can’t log the weight you used or see whether you did the workout last Tuesday.

Habit research points the same way. The [BPS Research Digest’s overview of habit formation](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) explains that habits form through repeating a behavior in a consistent context, which means a plan you can follow on the same days beats a bigger pile of options. ## A simple system for TikTok workout saves

You don’t need to stop saving. You need a short path from “saved” to “scheduled.” This four-step system takes about two minutes per video.

### Step 1: Triage once a week

Pick one day, open Favorites, and sort each new workout save into one of three buckets: do it this week, keep for later, or delete. Be honest. If you saved it for the music, it goes.

### Step 2: Turn the keepers into written routines

For every video in “do it this week,” write down each exercise, then the sets, reps and rest. If the video doesn’t give numbers, use a sensible default: 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps for strength moves, 30 to 45 seconds of work for timed moves, and 60 to 90 seconds of rest.

### Step 3: Put it on specific days

Match the routines to your week. The [CDC’s physical activity guidelines for adults](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days, so two or three short TikTok-style strength sessions plus some walking is a solid, realistic base. If you need help laying out the week, our guide to [workout reminders and scheduling](/blog/workout-reminders-and-scheduling) walks through it.

### Step 4: Log what you did

After each session, note the reps and any weight you used. That record is what tells you when to add a set, a few reps or a heavier dumbbell next time.

## Example: what a saved TikTok workout looks like as a routine

Here’s a typical 20-minute bodyweight video, the kind that racks up saves, written out as a routine you can actually run. Do 3 rounds, resting 90 seconds between rounds.

1. **Bodyweight squat:** 15 reps, controlled on the way down, 15 seconds rest
2. **Push-up (knees or full):** 8 to 12 reps, 15 seconds rest
3. **Reverse lunge:** 10 reps per leg, 15 seconds rest
4. **Glute bridge:** 15 reps with a one-second squeeze at the top, 15 seconds rest
5. **Plank shoulder tap:** 20 taps total, hips steady, 15 seconds rest
6. **Mountain climber:** 30 seconds at a steady pace, then 90 seconds rest before the next round

## Tips for saving fitness TikToks you’ll actually use

- **Save the full video, not the teaser.** Many creators post a “part 1.” Check their profile for the complete version before you save.
- **Read the caption.** Creators often put the set and rep scheme in the caption or a pinned comment rather than on screen.
- **Favor videos that show each exercise clearly.** You’ll learn the move faster and follow it with better form.
- **Keep one collection per goal.** “Lower body,” “Core,” “Mobility” and “Travel, no equipment” cover most people.
- **Copy the link for anything important.** Links are tiny, they work across apps, and they keep you pointed at the original creator.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can someone see when I save their TikTok video?

Adding a video to Favorites doesn’t send the creator a notification with your name. The save count on a post may go up, but it’s a number, not a list of people.

### Why can’t I save a TikTok video to my camera roll?

The creator has turned off downloads for that video or their account. You can still add it to Favorites or copy the link, which keeps it one tap away.

### How do I save a TikTok draft without posting it?

Drafts can’t be exported straight to your camera roll. Post the draft with privacy set to **Only me**, then open it and tap **Save video**. You can also switch on **Save to device** in **More options** before posting so a copy goes to your phone automatically.

### Where do saved TikTok videos go?

Favorites live in your TikTok profile under the bookmark tab. Downloaded videos go to the Photos app on iPhone or the Gallery app on Android, usually in your recent items.

### What’s the best way to save workout videos from TikTok?

Use Favorites to catch videos while you scroll, then turn the ones you want to do into a written routine with sets, reps and rest, and put that routine on specific days of your week.
