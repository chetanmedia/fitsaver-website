That is the job of a **workout split**: a simple weekly structure that decides what you train on each day, so every session has a purpose and every muscle gets enough work and enough rest. This guide explains how a split works, how to pick the right 3, 4, 5 or 6-day option for your schedule, and gives you a routine card for each one.

## What is a workout split?

A workout split is the way you divide your training across the week. Instead of doing everything every time you lift, you assign muscle groups or movement patterns to specific days. A full body split trains everything in each session. An upper/lower split alternates upper body days with lower body days. A push pull legs split groups chest, shoulders and triceps (push), back and biceps (pull), and legs on their own day.

The split itself does not build muscle. Consistent, progressive training does. What a good split does is make that consistency easier: you know what is coming, you can plan around busy days, and you avoid accidentally hammering the same muscles two days in a row.

### Why a split beats random workouts

- **Balanced volume.** Each muscle group gets a planned amount of work each week instead of whatever you felt like doing.
- **Built-in recovery.** Muscles you trained hard get a day or two off while you train something else.
- **Easier progress tracking.** When Monday is always upper body, you can compare this Monday’s numbers with last Monday’s.
- **Less decision fatigue.** You walk into the gym knowing the plan, which makes it much easier to start.

## How to pick the best workout split for you

There is no single best workout split. The right one is the split you can repeat for months. Work through these four questions in order and the answer usually becomes obvious.

### 1. How many days can you train, honestly?

Count the days you can train in a normal week, not your best week. If you plan for five and manage three, a five-day split leaves whole muscle groups untrained. A three-day plan that you complete every week will beat a six-day plan you finish half the time.

### 2. How experienced are you?

Beginners grow well on less volume and benefit from practicing the main lifts often, so full body or upper/lower splits are a strong start. Lifters with a year or more of steady training can usually handle, and often need, the extra volume that five and six-day splits provide.

### 3. What is your main goal?

For general health, the [CDC’s adult activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) recommend muscle-strengthening activity at least two days a week alongside regular aerobic activity, which a three-day split covers easily. For building muscle, how often you train each muscle matters: a [systematic review on training frequency](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) found that, with weekly volume equated, training a muscle group twice a week led to better growth than once a week. Every split in this guide hits each muscle at least twice.

### 4. How well do you recover?

Sleep, stress, job demands and other sports all eat into recovery. If you are on your feet all day or play a sport on weekends, a lower-frequency split with more rest days will usually feel and perform better.

## 3-day workout split: full body

**Best for:** beginners, busy weeks, and anyone getting back into training. You train three non-consecutive days, such as Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and each session covers the whole body with a squat or hinge, a push, a pull and some core work.

- **Monday:** Full body A
- **Wednesday:** Full body B
- **Friday:** Full body A (next week starts with B)

Because every muscle is trained three times a week, you get plenty of practice on the big lifts while keeping each session to about 45 to 60 minutes. If you miss a day, simply do the next session on your next free day.

### Routine card: Full body A

1. Goblet squat: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
2. Dumbbell bench press: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
3. Single-arm dumbbell row: 3 sets of 10 reps per side, rest 60 seconds
4. Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
5. Plank: 3 holds of 30 to 45 seconds, rest 45 seconds

## 4-day workout split: upper/lower

**Best for:** beginners ready for more, and intermediate lifters who want a balance of results and free time. You alternate upper body and lower body days, which trains every muscle twice a week with rest days in between. For full routine cards and two other ways to set up four days (body-part and full body A/B), see our [4 day workout split templates](/blog/4-day-workout-split-3-proven-templates-for-every-goal).

- **Monday:** Upper (strength focus)
- **Tuesday:** Lower (strength focus)
- **Thursday:** Upper (volume focus)
- **Friday:** Lower (volume focus)

Use heavier weights and lower reps (about 5 to 8) on the first two days, then lighter weights and higher reps (about 10 to 15) on the second pair. The split is flexible: if Wednesday works better than Tuesday, just keep upper and lower days alternating.

### Routine card: Upper (strength)

1. Barbell or dumbbell bench press: 4 sets of 6 reps, rest 2 minutes
2. Bent-over row: 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, rest 2 minutes
3. Overhead press: 3 sets of 8 reps, rest 90 seconds
4. Lat pulldown or pull-ups: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
5. Dumbbell curl and triceps pushdown: 2 sets of 12 reps each, rest 60 seconds

## 5-day workout split: upper/lower plus push pull legs

**Best for:** intermediate lifters who enjoy training most weekdays and want extra volume. A popular structure combines an upper/lower pair with a push pull legs block, so each muscle is still trained about twice a week.

- **Monday:** Upper
- **Tuesday:** Lower
- **Wednesday:** Rest
- **Thursday:** Push (chest, shoulders, triceps)
- **Friday:** Pull (back, biceps)
- **Saturday:** Legs

The classic “bro split,” where each muscle group gets one day a week (chest day, back day and so on), is also a five-day option. It can work if you enjoy it, but it trains each muscle only once a week, so the hybrid above is usually the better choice for steady growth.

### Routine card: Push

1. Incline dumbbell press: 4 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
2. Seated dumbbell shoulder press: 3 sets of 10 reps, rest 90 seconds
3. Cable or machine chest fly: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds
4. Lateral raise: 3 sets of 15 reps, rest 45 seconds
5. Overhead triceps extension: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds

## 6-day workout split: push pull legs twice

**Best for:** experienced lifters with time to train six days and the recovery to match. You run push, pull and legs, then repeat, with one full rest day.

- **Monday:** Push
- **Tuesday:** Pull
- **Wednesday:** Legs
- **Thursday:** Push
- **Friday:** Pull
- **Saturday:** Legs
- **Sunday:** Rest

Because sessions are close together, keep individual workouts shorter and manage effort. Leave one or two reps in reserve on most sets, and if your lifts stall or you feel run down for more than a week, drop to the 4 or 5-day split for a while.

### Routine card: Legs

1. Back squat: 4 sets of 6 to 8 reps, rest 2 to 3 minutes
2. Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps, rest 2 minutes
3. Walking lunge: 3 sets of 10 steps per leg, rest 90 seconds
4. Leg curl: 3 sets of 12 reps, rest 60 seconds
5. Standing calf raise: 4 sets of 12 to 15 reps, rest 45 seconds

## Workout split comparison at a glance

- **3 days, full body:** each muscle trained 3 times a week, about 45 to 60 minutes per session, best for beginners and busy schedules.
- **4 days, upper/lower:** each muscle trained twice a week, about 50 to 70 minutes, best all-round choice for most people.
- **5 days, hybrid:** each muscle trained about twice a week, about 50 to 70 minutes, best for intermediates who want more volume.
- **6 days, push pull legs:** each muscle trained twice a week, about 45 to 75 minutes, best for experienced lifters with good recovery.

Core work slots into any of these. Add two or three ab exercises at the end of two sessions a week; our ranking of the [best ab workout exercises](/blog/best-ab-workout-exercises-ranked) is a good place to choose them.

## Common workout split mistakes

- **Picking the split for the week you wish you had.** Choose for your average week, then add days later.
- **Changing splits every two weeks.** Give a split at least 8 to 12 weeks so you can see real progress.
- **Ignoring progression.** Add a rep or a little weight when you hit the top of a rep range. A split without progression is just a schedule.
- **Skipping legs or pulling work.** Keep the week balanced between pushing, pulling and lower body.
- **No plan for missed days.** Decide in advance: either push the week back a day or skip the missed session and carry on.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best workout split for beginners?

A 3-day full body split or a 4-day upper/lower split. Both train every muscle at least twice a week, keep sessions manageable and give you plenty of practice on the main lifts.

### Is a 3-day split enough to build muscle?

Yes. Three full body sessions a week train each muscle three times, which is plenty for most people as long as you add reps or weight over time and train close to failure on your working sets.

### How long should I stay on one workout split?

Run it for at least 8 to 12 weeks. Switch when your schedule changes, when progress has stalled for several weeks despite good sleep and effort, or when you are ready for more training days.

### Can I do cardio on a workout split?

Yes. Add cardio on rest days or after lifting. Keep hard intervals away from the day before heavy leg sessions so your legs are fresh when they need to be.

### What if I miss a day on my split?

Do the missed session on your next training day and shift the rest of the week by one day. If that is not possible, skip it and continue as planned. One missed day will not undo your progress.
