An upper ab workout is built around one movement: curling your rib cage down toward your pelvis. That’s the job of the top portion of your rectus abdominis, the long “six-pack” muscle that runs down the front of your torso. You can’t switch off the lower part while you train the top, but you can choose exercises that put most of the work up there. This guide covers six moves that do exactly that, how to do each one well, and a simple routine card you can run two or three times a week.

## What the upper abs actually are

Your rectus abdominis is one muscle, divided by bands of connective tissue into the segments people call a six-pack. When people say “upper abs,” they mean the top segments, just below the chest.

Because it is one muscle, every ab exercise works the whole thing to some degree. What changes is the emphasis. Movements that start at the top, where your shoulders and rib cage curl down while your hips stay still, tend to be felt most in the upper section. Movements where your legs and pelvis move toward a fixed upper body tend to be felt lower down.

Two practical takeaways:

- **Pick top-down moves.** Crunch variations, cable crunches and rollouts load the upper section well.
- **Visible upper abs come down to body fat.** Training builds the muscle. Seeing it depends mostly on overall body fat, which is driven by diet, sleep and total activity, not ab work alone.

## The 6 best upper ab workout exercises

Each move below includes the setup, the key cue and a common mistake. Start with the easier options and progress when you can finish every set with clean form.

### 1. Crunch

The basic crunch is still one of the most direct upper ab exercises. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Lightly touch your fingertips behind your ears or cross your arms over your chest.

- **Cue:** Think “ribs to hips.” Lift your shoulder blades off the floor by curling your spine, not by pulling your head.
- **Range:** Your lower back stays on the floor. The movement is short, about 30 degrees.
- **Common mistake:** Yanking the neck forward. Keep a fist-sized gap between chin and chest.

### 2. Toe-touch crunch

Lie on your back with your legs pointing up toward the ceiling, knees slightly soft. Reach both hands toward your toes, lifting your shoulder blades off the floor, then lower with control. Holding your legs in the air keeps the lower body still, so the top of your abs does the work.

- **Cue:** Reach up, not forward. Imagine touching the ceiling just past your feet.
- **Common mistake:** Swinging the arms for momentum. Pause for a beat at the top of each rep.

### 3. Stability ball crunch

Sit on a stability ball and walk your feet forward until your lower back rests on the ball. Let your upper back extend slightly over the curve, then crunch up. Starting from a gentle stretch gives you more range than a floor crunch. In an [ACE-sponsored study of popular ab exercises](https://www.acefitness.org/about-ace/press-room/press-releases/246/ace-sponsored-study-reveals-best-and-worst-abdominal-exercises/), the exercise-ball crunch produced more rectus abdominis activity than the standard floor crunch.

- **Cue:** Keep your hips level and still. Only your upper body moves.
- **Progression:** Hold a light dumbbell or plate against your chest.

### 4. Decline crunch

On a decline bench, hook your feet under the pads and lie back. Curl up until your torso is roughly halfway, then lower slowly. The decline adds resistance at the top of the movement, right where the upper abs work hardest.

- **Cue:** Curl your spine one segment at a time instead of hinging at the hips.
- **Common mistake:** Sitting all the way up. That shifts the work to the hip flexors.

### 5. Kneeling cable crunch

Attach a rope to a high cable pulley and kneel facing the stack, holding the rope beside your head. Keep your hips stacked over your knees and crunch down, bringing your elbows toward your thighs. Because you can add weight in small steps, this is one of the easiest ways to apply progressive overload to your abs.

- **Cue:** Your hips don’t move. If your hips sit back toward your heels, the weight is too heavy.
- **Home option:** Anchor a resistance band high on a door and do the same movement.

### 6. Ab wheel rollout

Kneel on a mat holding an ab wheel under your shoulders. Brace your abs, then roll forward as far as you can while keeping your lower back from sagging. Pull back to the start using your abs. The rollout trains your abs to resist arching, and most people feel it strongly in the upper section.

![Woman doing an ab wheel rollout on a gym floor](https://framerusercontent.com/images/PrczOE3YhDRIgaHL2Hj3fx62Smc.jpg)

- **Cue:** Tuck your pelvis slightly and keep your ribs down the whole time.
- **Regression:** Roll toward a wall so it stops you at a short range, then move farther away over the weeks.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips drop. End the rep before your back arches.

## How to program an upper ab workout

Abs recover like other muscles. Two or three focused sessions a week, with at least a day between them, is plenty for most people. You can run this routine as a finisher after an upper-body or full-body session, or on its own on a lighter day. That lines up with the [CDC’s physical activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html), which recommend muscle-strengthening work for all major muscle groups, including the abs, at least two days a week.

### Sets, reps and progression

- **Reps:** 10 to 20 per set for bodyweight moves, 10 to 15 for weighted moves like the cable crunch.
- **Rest:** 30 to 60 seconds between sets.
- **Progression:** When you hit the top of the rep range on every set with clean form, add reps, slow the lowering phase to three seconds, or add a little weight.

### Pair it with the rest of your core

Upper ab work is one piece of the puzzle. For a balanced core, add a lower ab move (like reverse crunches or hanging knee raises) and an anti-rotation move (like a side plank or Pallof press). Our [ranked list of the best ab workout exercises](/blog/best-ab-workout-exercises-ranked) covers those in more detail. [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/want-a-stronger-core-skip-the-sit-ups) also points to planks and other hold-style moves as a back-friendly way to train the whole core.

## Upper ab workout routine card

Here’s a 15 to 20 minute upper ab routine. Do it two or three times a week. The gym version is listed first, with home swaps in brackets.

1. **Crunch:** 3 sets of 15 reps, 30 seconds rest
2. **Toe-touch crunch:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 30 seconds rest
3. **Stability ball crunch:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 45 seconds rest \[home: floor crunch with a 2-second pause at the top\]
4. **Decline crunch:** 2 sets of 10 to 12 reps, 45 seconds rest \[home: crunch with your calves resting on a couch\]
5. **Kneeling cable crunch:** 3 sets of 12 reps, 60 seconds rest \[home: band crunch anchored on a door\]
6. **Ab wheel rollout:** 3 sets of 6 to 10 reps, 60 seconds rest \[home: wall-stop rollout or plank walkout\]

Warm up first with a couple of minutes of easy movement, such as marching in place and cat-cow stretches. Stop any exercise that causes pain, and keep the lowering phase slow on every rep.

## Common upper ab workout mistakes

- **Pulling on your neck.** Your hands support your head, they don’t lift it. If your neck gets tired before your abs, cross your arms instead.
- **Going too fast.** Quick, bouncy reps run on momentum. A slow lowering phase is where a lot of the work happens.
- **Training abs every day.** More isn’t better. Give them rest days like any other muscle group.
- **Expecting spot reduction.** Ab exercises strengthen the muscle. They don’t burn fat from your stomach specifically.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you really target your upper abs?

You can emphasize them. The rectus abdominis is one muscle, so it always works as a unit, but top-down moves like crunches and cable crunches place more of the load on the upper section.

### How often should I do an upper ab workout?

Two or three times a week is enough for most people. Leave at least one day between sessions so the muscles can recover.

### Why can I see my upper abs but not my lower abs?

Many people carry more fat around the lower belly, so the upper section tends to show first. That’s about where your body stores fat, not weak lower abs. A balanced core routine and overall consistency are the best approach.

### Do I need equipment for an upper ab workout?

No. Crunches and toe-touch crunches need only a mat. Equipment like a cable machine or ab wheel makes it easier to add resistance and keep progressing over time.

### Are crunches bad for your back?

For most healthy people, crunches done with control and a short range are fine. If you have a back injury or ongoing pain, check with a qualified health professional before adding them.
